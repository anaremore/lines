## Invariants (enforced by tests)

- Prices are vendor-neutral: `Price(i64)` is an integer count of the *catalog product's* price unit, whose size is an exact rational `PriceScale` defined once in `catalog.rs` (ES/NQ/MES/MNQ: 1/100, tick 25 units). Quantities are `Qty(i64)`, a count of `10^-decimals` of the base unit (futures: decimals 0). No core type is in any vendor's units.
- Each provider converts its wire values exactly at its edge. CQG converts `scaled_price` with `UnitConverter`, from `correct_price_scale` turned into an exact rational. Coinbase parses decimal text, and REST numbers via `RawValue`. Sierra converts `f32 / multiplier` with an f32-relative tolerance, and the result must be on the tick grid. Values that aren't exactly representable are counted (`MarketEvent::Rejected`, import report), never rounded. CQG metadata whose tick disagrees with the catalog is a fatal `BadMetadata`.
- Instruments (decisions 0013, 0028): a `ContractKey` is a product plus a `Delivery`: a futures month, spot (crypto: one perpetual contract), an equity's shares, or a listed option `{ expiry, strike, right }` on the *underlying's* product, the strike an exact `Price` in its price units. The multiplier (100), exercise style and option tick are the product's `OptionRules`, never in the key, so one OCC symbol is one key. `Product::lists` says whether a key's kind fits its product. Option money is premium units × multiplier (`ContractSpec`). OCC symbols (`lines_feed::occ`, the 21-character OSI form) are the industry's standard, derived from the key by rule like futures symbols, so they are public; a strike OCC can't write exactly has no symbol.
- Vendor symbols exist only inside `lines-feed` (the crate-private `catalog::roots` table and `src/{cqg,coinbase,kraken}`) and the dev-only `lines-sierra`. The UI sees `Product`, `ContractKey`, `Capabilities` and the provider id. Bid/ask display is capability-gated.
- `ProviderSession`s do no I/O. HTTP responses arrive as `FrameKind::Http` frames carrying `URL
body` (pre-URL recordings: body only), so recordings contain them and sessions interpret a response by its URL, not their own request plan. Recordings hold inbound frames whole and outbound frames as their time only (no data: a logon carries credentials; replay skips them and needs only their time, its start clock; older files hold their bytes), and `LogonParams`' `Debug` shows only whether a credential is set. Replay drives the same session code; its clock is the recorded frame time (the first recorded frame before any delivery).
- Option chains (decision 0028, `lines_core::chain`): a provider streams one `MarketEvent::OptionQuote { key, quote }` per series on its underlying's feed; the store keeps them in `ChartData::chains` (per expiry, strikes ascending; a quote replaces the series' whole quote) with their own revision, never moving `chart_epoch` (charts don't repaint for chain quotes) nor counting as the feed's updates; at most 400 expiries and 2,000 strikes per expiry are kept (more are counted as rejected). Quote prices are exact; IV and delta are display-only fixed-point integers (hundredths of a percent, ten-thousandths), so quotes compare exactly and replays are deterministic. In `lines-events-1` an option quote is tag 5, on the recording's own product; a product without listed options rejects it (counted). The chain pane (`chain_view`, a pane kind since workspace format 6) repaints on the store's revision, copies the rows in view under a `try_lock` into a fixed buffer (a busy store redraws the last frame) and paints every cell into a stack string (no allocation), mirrors the columns around the strike (calls: OI, volume, IV, delta, last, bid, ask; puts the reverse from bid), leaves out whole columns from the outside in on a narrow pane (bid, ask and the strike always stay), draws the underlying's price as a row of its own between the strikes it falls between, and shades each side's in-the-money cells. It skips a frame whose bounds are empty (a first frame can be empty and far off: stepping a float across it never ends).
- Paper options (decision 0028, phase 3; `paper.rs`, `risk::check_option`, `core::settle_options`): the feed bridges forward each option quote to its own series (`MarketInput::OptionQuote`; a store's chains seed the core when it starts following). An option fills only on its own quote, as a one-level book of unlimited size: an order crossing it takes it at the quote's price; a resting limit fills at its limit when a later quote goes through it (touch: also at it); prints on the option or its underlying never fill. A missing side (none, zero, or a crossed quote) is empty, so nothing fills on it, and a market order (Flatten) is cancelled rather than filling at a last price. A quote older than `PaperConfig::option_quote_stale_ms` fills nothing: an order that would cross it rests, a market order is cancelled. Orders and fills only in the regular session. Before the broker, the core refuses option orders on a non-simulated broker (live is phase 4, an owner decision), on an expired series and on a prop-rule account (prop rules don't apply: such an account opens none, and the contract cap doesn't count options), and the guard (`RiskLimits::option_guard`, default when absent, never off) refuses market orders unless allowed, every stop and bracket, and a limit farther from the mid than its percentage or tick floor, on placing and moving it. Without a two-sided fresh quote there is no mid, so a limit is refused: typed blind, it could fill far from the market on the next quote. The one exception is a reduce-only limit at the side of a fresh quote that closes it (`risk::check_option_exit`), which needs no mid and no other side. A quote older than the newest is ignored, and an empty one keeps the last mark. A quote stamped ahead of the clock counts as stamped now, so it still goes stale and freezes nothing. Guardrails apply as to any order (an option's close is the regular session's). Flatten and cancels pass the guard; Flatten is never sent for an option on a non-simulated broker, and setting prop rules is refused while the account holds options or option orders. At the expiry's regular close (`option_close`) the core cancels the series' orders and settles positions at intrinsic against the underlying's last price before the close, through `Broker::settle` (no fee; simulated brokers only; one try per run); the service ticks before a batch reaches the marks, so a print the clock has already passed never counts. With no underlying price from the 24 hours before the close the position waits for the next price and settles on that.
- Option triggers (decision 0029; `crate::trigger`, `core/trigger.rs`): the core holds them and only a trade of the underlying in the option's regular hours fires one, so a stopped feed can't, a print more than `OptionGuard::trigger_stale_ms` old can't, and `TriggerView::stale` reports it. They issue ordinary commands as `Source::Rule`, past the same gates: an entry is one `PlaceOrder` limit at the ask (every new-order check at that moment; a refusal or an ask over the cap ends it), a fired Stop is `Flatten` of the series (why it may be a market order, and why it can't open, grow or flip), a fired Target is `PlaceExit` plus `ModifyOrder` at most every `target_chase_ms`. Exits take the position's size when they act and end when it goes flat or flips. A print after a gap (stale, or the market opening) past a level fires an exit and ends an entry. A person's Cancel All or Flatten ends the triggers it covers. Every `rebuild` publishes the live ones, so a restored exit's notice reaches a subscriber that wasn't listening yet. Saved: Stops and Targets only (trading file v4), held by `restore_triggers` until the first `rebuild` brings the positions back. A fired exit's commands pass the account's risk limits too (`Triggers::exempt`): they block entries, never exits. A fired exit ends its partner and any entry armed on the series. Triggers are refused on prop-rule accounts, venues and strategies. They and strategies never share a contract, so a trigger never trades under a strategy: a trigger won't arm where an instance is armed, paused or holds its own position, and an instance won't arm where a trigger is alive. A fired entry's resting limit is an order like a person's: an instance armed after it can have a fill it doesn't own.
- The chain's ticket (`chain_ticket.rs` builds and checks orders with no UI; `chain_view.rs` draws it): nothing moves a typed or defaulted limit once shown, so the send button always says what Enter sends. The ticket asks the same check as the core (`check_option`, or `check_option_exit` for a close) before sending, with the account's own guard (`AccountInfo::option_guard`), and shows the core's words; the core still decides. A row's Close and a ticket in Close mode are `PlaceExit` only (reduce-only, held to the position). Flatten and Cancel are the DOM's commands and keys, one per series of the underlying the account has anything on (every expiry, nothing else), never gated by the feed, quote or One-click; a read-only account alone refuses them. They read the cache with `try_lock` like the rest of the pane: busy, they say so and the press is repeated. One-click is off in every new pane, never saved, and off again on a symbol or account change; only a single click in the already focused pane sends. Enter only opens the ticket or sends what it shows, and a held Enter does neither (`workspace.rs`'s rule for orders). A click that closes the picker presses nothing. Accepted isn't filled: the pane watches its orders and shows a broker's rejection. The rows don't scroll while the selected strike is in view, and with One-click on the same strikes stay in the same places until One-click goes off, Home or another expiry: `Frame` holds the strikes and the price row's slot, the view pins the expiry, quotes are looked up each frame, and keys move only among those strikes, so a newly listed strike isn't shown or reached until then. A press lands where it was aimed. The Pos column, the header, the strip's slot and the footer keep their sizes for the same reason: an order landing moves no row.
- `.lfr` is format **v3**, or **v4** only when the contract is an equity or option (decision 0028; the contract kinds are all v4 adds): header = provider, protocol, catalog product symbol, contract key. A futures or spot recording is byte-for-byte what earlier builds wrote, so they still replay it; a v3 file claiming an equity or option contract is damaged. v2 files still read, with the contract unknown (shown as "contract unknown"; asking a v2 file for a specific contract is an error). v1 is rejected. Replay refuses unknown providers or protocols and product/contract mismatches. A recording holds exactly one contract: if the front month rolls mid-recording, recording stops. Imported data uses provider `lines` / protocol `lines-events-1`. Each frame is one atomic batch, depth included, so the store applies it under one lock and checks for a crossed book only at frame boundaries.
- Sierra import is deterministic (same inputs → byte-identical file). Trades are checked against the book at emit time, never against look-ahead state. Sweep fills (Sierra unbundled trades) are not expected at the touch.
- Products vs contracts (decision 0013): the store is keyed by contract (`ChartData::contract`). `MarketEvent::ContractChanged(key)` retires the old contract's bars (kept, unadjusted, for continuous charts), never mixes them. Front month = `roll::resolve_front`: volume crossover when prior-session volumes are given, else the product's calendar rule, never backwards, never an expired contract. Live feeds following a product re-resolve on every (re)connect. Nothing rolls positions.
- Trading sessions (decision 0017, `lines_core::sessions`): a session belongs to its trade date; the ETH session of trade date D opens at `eth_open` on the calendar day before D (CME's Sunday 17:00 CT open is Monday's session) and closes on D; with same-day hours (`eth_open` before `eth_close`: US equities, 04:00–20:00 ET) it opens and closes on D, and halt days are refused (closed or early only). Hours are exchange-local in an IANA zone (jiff, bundled tzdb, so results never depend on the machine). Holidays/early closes are versioned data (`lines-feed/data/exchange-holidays.txt`, format 1, every date `verified` or `unverified` with sources); a `halt` holiday is not a trade date and its morning belongs to the next trade date's session (`Session::halt`), at most one per gap. Outside a table's coverage weekdays are assumed regular and sessions report `HolidayData::NotCovered`. Session queries never allocate (`lines-core/tests/sessions_no_alloc.rs`).
- Continuous series are display-only (`continuous::build`): unadjusted by default, difference back-adjust as an option (exact, on the tick grid), no ratio adjustment.
- Sierra contract from the file name: 2-digit years are 20YY; a 1-digit year is the earliest matching year not before the file's last record.
- History + live stitch (`lines_core::stitch`, roadmap 4): a history snapshot is bars plus a *closed* `as_of` (includes every trade with `time <= as_of`, none after; CQG's `up_to_utc_time` definition). Live trades with `time <= as_of` are dropped as already counted; later ones are added; trades applied before a late snapshot overwrote their bar are re-applied from a bounded journal (500k trades while history is outstanding, then the newest 3 bars). Open/close follow trade time, not arrival order. Providers must supply a closed `as_of`: CQG `up_to_utc_time` (the mock never stamps a trade at or before its last snapshot); Coinbase candles closed at the bucket end, the forming bar rebuilt from REST trades de-duplicated against websocket trades by `trade_id`. REST lags the websocket by seconds, so id holes (REST newest .. `last_match`, and matches dropped mid-stream) are fetched by id (`trades?after=hi+1`), retried on the keepalive tick, and reported as a history gap after 6 attempts; a hole wider than 6 full pages (6,000 ids) is a gap at once, and holes shrink by the ids received, never by walking id space; ids 0 and `u64::MAX` are malformed (rejected), so id arithmetic never wraps; history is `Complete` only once the REST-to-websocket hole is closed. Every (re)connect requests history from the newest stored bar; the window is `FeedConfig::history_ms` (default 3 days). `ChartData::history` (`Loading`/`Complete`/`Incomplete`/`Unsupported`) is shown as header text.
- All CQG `sint64` times are ms offsets from `LogonResult.base_time`.
- Depth (roadmap 5): `MarketEvent::BookClear`/`BookLevel` (new total per price, 0 removes); one frame is one atomic batch. CQG subscribes at `LEVEL_TRADES_BBA_DOM` and maps `TYPE_BID`/`TYPE_ASK` quotes; a snapshot clears the book. The CQG mock keeps a 10-level, one-tick-wide book that shifts with its price walk and is never crossed at a frame boundary. Coinbase uses the public `level2_batch` channel (`level2` needs authentication). Kraken spot subscribes `book` at depth 100; the session keeps its own book truncated to that depth (Kraken expects clients to drop levels pushed past it) and checks Kraken's CRC-32 of the top 10 levels after *every* message (each field is the value in grid units, so the checksum string is integer digits, never a float). A mismatch is counted, clears the book at once (`BookClear`; a book known to be wrong is never shown), and resubscribes; updates are ignored until the fresh snapshot. Kraken Futures books have no checksum: their sequence number must advance by one, and a skip is the same clear-and-resync (counted as a resync, not a `Gap`: `Gap` means dropped trades to the store and the local data store). `FeedStats::{book_checksums_ok, book_checksum_mismatches, book_resyncs}` count all of it. Kraken sessions validate tick and lot size against Kraken's public metadata (`AssetPairs`, Futures `instruments`) before subscribing: a mismatch is a fatal `BadMetadata`. Kraken Futures candle *volumes* are float sums printed to 16 digits (`0.3359999999999999`); only they are snapped, and only within 1e-6 of a grid step (`kraken::num::units_from_float_text`); everything else Kraken sends is parsed exactly from its text. Trades carry `aggressor: Option<Aggressor>`: CQG `sales_condition` (HIT/SELL_SIDE → sell, TAKE/BUY_SIDE → buy), Coinbase from the maker side (opposite), Sierra from the import.
- The store's `tape` (8192 prints) and `vap` (volume at price since data start for the contract) take only trades the stitch counts as new, so a re-delivered trade is never counted twice. Coinbase delivers the forming bar's REST trades as a closed snapshot (every REST trade before the newest REST timestamp `t_n`, `as_of = t_n - 1 ms`; trades at `t_n` follow as trades), so a reconnect inside the same minute replaces the bar instead of adding to it.
- DOM ladder (`lines_core::ladder`): rows are integer ticks × grouping (`row = floor(price / (grouping × tick))`); grouping only sums exact `Qty`, never rounds. `build_ladder` scans only the visible price range into a fixed-capacity buffer: per frame it visits at most `3 × rows × grouping` book/volume levels whatever the book depth (`tests/depth_stress.rs` asserts this at max replay speed, plus builds paced by the 8 ms UI tick, not the feed). Centring follows the inside market when it leaves the middle half of the rows, never while the pointer is over the ladder; C re-centres. Locked/crossed books are labelled and outlined, not only tinted.
- Panes (`lines-app/src/pane.rs`): chart, DOM and T&S are independent; each is given (`PaneFeed`, theme), owns its view state, polls only its own store's revision and is rendered as a cached view, so a tick on one market repaints only the panes showing it. Charts and T&S poll `ChartStore::chart_revision`, which depth below the touch doesn't move (a busy book repaints only the DOM). Panes never reference each other; a symbol switch emits `FeedChanged` and the workspace forwards it to the pane's link group. Feeds are shared through `FeedRegistry` keyed by (source, product, contract, follows front month); the last `Subscription` dropped stops the feed on a background thread. The layout is a value (`layout::Layout<PaneId>`; saved as `Layout<PaneDoc>`), rendered from `Layout::place` so drawing and hit-testing share one geometry.
- Workspaces (decision 0016): workspace → windows → tabs → layout → panes. Link groups (A–D, colour + letter, or unlinked) span all windows; joining a group adopts its market. Only the active tab of a visible window is rendered; every other pane is marked hidden and its poll doesn't notify, so hidden tabs and minimised windows do no paint work (`--bench` exits 2 if its hidden tab painted). Focus is always drawn (violet outline; dim when the window is inactive). Keys go through one capture-phase handler: `commands::command_for` (pure, tested) maps them to `Command`s, which the context menu also issues; pane keys (letters, C/G, Home, Ctrl+K, Ctrl+D, Alt+M) are never bound. Crosshair sync (opt-in per workspace, Ctrl+Shift+X) is keyed by link group (`tabstate::crosshair_peers`: same group, unlinked with unlinked): a chart emits `CrosshairTime` (time, price, product) after paint; its peers mark that bar (`geometry::time_slot`, missing in a gap). Optional price sync (`crosshair_sync_price`, palette/tab menu) also marks the price, only between charts of the same product (`tabstate::syncs_price`).
- Hide / detach (roadmap 5b): a hidden or detached pane keeps its leaf, so its tab's layout keeps every ratio and showing or re-attaching restores the exact size and position (`Layout::place_where` gives a wholly hidden side's space to its sibling). Hidden panes keep their feed, link and settings and paint nothing (`--bench-hide-doms`). A detached pane stays owned by its tab (same link group, subscriptions) and is rendered by a `PaneWindow`; closing that window re-attaches it, never loses it. "Chart only" hides a chart's linked DOM/T&S in its tab as `chart_only` and restores exactly those; panes the trader hid stay hidden; detached ones are untouched. A tab always keeps one shown pane. A window shows its tab bar only with two tabs or more (`tabstate::shows_tab_bar`); the workspace status (name, crosshair sync, report, hidden/popped-out panes) is always in the footer, and Ctrl+T, the pane menu and the palette make a second tab. Closed tabs go on an in-memory stack (10) as their saved form (feeds stop) and Reopen brings them back at their index; closing the last tab of the last window replaces it with a fresh tab. Footer notices (`notice.rs`): the same message replaces itself with a count (`×3`); refusals expire after 6 s (one timer each, nothing ticking); problems that need action (files unreadable/unsaved) stay until clicked. Hover controls (pane header, tab ×) are built only for the pane/tab under the pointer, found from geometry on mouse move: the window root re-renders every frame, so per-pane chrome costs every frame (this was measured: ~25% on the 6+2 benchmark).
- Table columns (DOM, T&S, journal; the journal leaves out, for the frame, columns a narrow pane can't hold whole): each pane paints a header row; its dividers resize (drag; double-click fits the column to its widest current content) and right-click opens a window-wide column menu (show/hide, fit, reset; the price column always stays; the DOM's orders column is hidden until trading exists). Layout is `lines_core::columns` (pure, fixed arrays, covered by `no_alloc.rs`): a column is never narrower than its title or widest current number (measured each frame from cached glyphs), and one column takes the leftover width (DOM volume, T&S time; else the last shown). Drags are absolute (start width + pointer offset) and the divider follows the pointer: the column resized is the one on the divider's anchored side (left of the fill column: the left one; right of it, as in T&S: the right one), and it grows only as far as the fill column can give way (tested for every divider, both ways, both tables). Every action is `Command::Columns` (palette too). Saved per pane as optional `columns` maps holding only what differs from the defaults (no version bump).
- Unservable panes (`feeds::Fallback`, decided once at startup): when the source serves exactly one contract (a replay) and no `--instrument` was given, a pane whose symbol it can't serve adopts that contract for the run (`feeds::resolve`, pure); its saved target is untouched, so autosave keeps the workspace's symbol, and its header says "replay: NQU25 (workspace: ES)". Otherwise an unavailable pane offers a one-click switch to the source's symbol (replay contract, else default product): a button plus Enter, `Command::ShowSourceHere`; saved like a picker switch. When every pane of a tab (two or more) is unavailable, the tab shows one "Switch all panes" banner instead (`tabstate::switch_offer`, `Command::ShowSourceInTab`).
- Diagnostics (roadmap U1): `logs` writes `tracing` to stderr and, for the interactive app only (never headless, screenshot or benchmark runs), to rotating files `<data dir>/logs/lines.log`, `lines.1.log`… (default 5 × 10 MB; `LINES_LOG_DIR`, `LINES_LOG_FILES` 1–50, `LINES_LOG_MB` 1–1000) on its own thread (lines dropped and counted, never blocking). Every line is redacted (`redact::text`) before it reaches the disk; a panic hook writes the panic and a backtrace synchronously. `LINES_BUILD_REV` (build.rs: env, else `git rev-parse` + `-dirty`) is logged at start. "Export diagnostics…" (palette, pane and tab menus) writes one zip (`zipw`, deflate on flate2 + crc32fast) to a folder the user picks (default Downloads) off the UI thread and shows its path: README, `about.json` (version, build rev, OS, GPU adapter, scale, displays), `logs/`, `config/` (settings and workspaces, redacted by key with `redact::json`; maps keyed by account, `guardrails`, `bracket_presets` and `prop_rules`, lose their keys; every new account-keyed map must be added to `redact::ACCOUNT_KEYED`), `feeds.json` (per feed over the last 5 minutes, sampled every 5 s: updates/frames per second, gaps, rejects, reconnects, checksum mismatches, resyncs), `frames.json` (per pane paints/s, chart paint µs). Nothing is uploaded; level data is never included. `diagnostics::tests::no_secret_survives_into_a_bundle` plants fake credentials, account ids, keys and e-mails in settings, workspaces and logs and checks none survive. Never log credentials, account ids, API keys or level values from paid sources; redaction is the second line of defence, not a licence. Text redaction (`redact::text`, logs and every bundle file) also covers the user's name in home-folder paths (any case; a name with spaces runs to the next slash), an `Authorization` header (any key holding the word) to the end of its line and the word after `Bearer`/`Basic`/`Digest` anywhere, and names ending in `key`, `sig`, `sign` or `signature` (`access_key`, `X-Amz-Signature`; not `hotkey`, `design`, `assign`, `monkey`, `turnkey`), plus `nonce`.
- Saved files (`settings.json`, `workspace.json`, `workspaces/<name>.json` under `persist::config_dir`, `LINES_CONFIG_DIR` overrides) carry `"version"`. Reads go through `versioned::load`: older versions migrate forward (the old file is kept as `<stem>.v<N>.json`), newer or unreadable files are copied aside (`<stem>.v<N>-<ms>.json`, `<stem>.unreadable-<ms>.json`) and reported inline, then defaults load; a file that can't be backed up is never saved over. Saves are atomic with `<file>.bak`, on the `persist::Writer` thread (never the UI thread). Workspace format is v7 (v2: pane `state`, detached `window`; v3: the `journal` pane kind; v4: the `strategies` pane kind; v5: equity and option contracts in a pane's target; v6: the `chain` pane kind; v7: a chain's `chain_chart`; each new kind or mode bumps the version so an older build reports the file as newer instead of failing on it); v1 to v6 migrate (tested, pinned; a v4 file's futures and spot contracts read back as the same keys); changing it needs a version bump and a migration. Screenshots and the benchmark read and write no user files.
- Chart layers (`chart_view::ChartLayer`): `prepare` under the store lock after the viewport update, `paint` inside the plot after the candles; neither may allocate. Paint order in the plot: grid, volume profile rows and the levels' back zones (behind the candles), candles (minus the bars the footprint draws as cells), `layers`, indicator studies (VWAP lines), footprint cells (opaque, so a profile row or study line passes under a cell, never through its numbers; each column's open → close strip with a wedge marking the open on its left), the levels' front lines and labels, the profile's label, last-price line, crosshair. The footprint (`footprint::FootprintLayer`) is held typed like the profile, because the candle loop asks it which bars it covers (`covers`, indexed from the visible range's first bar, the same range the candle scene uses). The volume profile (`lines-app/src/profile.rs`) implements `ChartLayer` but is held typed by `ChartView` (its rows paint before the candles, its label after the studies); it is not an indicator-engine node: the engine's series are per-bar sums and lines, not per-price volume, and visible-range mode depends on the viewport.
- Indicators (decision 0017): every feed's batches reach the indicator thread through `indicators::EngineTap` (beside the store writer); the engine runs `lines_indicators::Graph` in feed order (deterministic). A feed's base series (`Spec::Market`) lives as long as its `ChartStore` (a finished replay can still get indicators). Identical (feed, spec) requests share one node. Consumers get an `IndicatorHandle` whose `OutputCell` the engine updates in place (only changed points) with an atomic revision; panes poll it via `Pane::changed` and `try_lock` in paint. VWAP: accumulators are exact `i128` (`3pv`, `9p²v`, `v`, so bar typical prices `(H+L+C)/3` stay integer); a bucket is exact only if its trades all arrived as trades (after the newest snapshot's `as_of`, or from a replay's first recorded frame (`Input::TradesFrom`), no gap/disconnect), else bar-derived and the point is flagged approximate until the next reset; at startup the engine also gets the store's cached bars (`Input::Cached`, bar-derived) and its stored trades with their coverage (`Input::Stored`: trade sums only, never bars; the covered ranges are complete); the value is `f64`, never an order price. Resets come from the sessions calendar (ETH default, RTH, week, month of trade dates) or an anchor bar. Drawn by `studies.rs` beside the `ChartLayer`s (same prepare/paint points; the trait is unchanged) as a smooth anti-aliased path through bar centres plus band polygons; GPUI tessellates those paths per frame (its allocation), the column geometry is allocation-free. A chart's indicators are saved in its `ChartState.indicators` (optional, empty by default). Screenshot mode waits for the engine (`engine().sync()`) before drawing.
- Footprint (roadmap 10e, `lines_core::footprint`, app `footprint.rs`): a chart bar's cells are the merge of the store's 1-minute footprints over its bucket `[bar.time, BarsView::end(i))`, never a separate per-timeframe store, so every timeframe sums the same trades (tested against footprints counted directly at 5m/15m/1h). Trade-built bars (tick, volume, range, seconds) are the exception: their bucket boundaries fall inside minutes, so each such bar keeps its own per-price volume from the same counted trades (`footprint::bar_levels`, tested against a footprint counted per bar). Counted trades only. POC uses `ProfileRows::build` (the profile's tie rule); imbalances compare diagonally (`buy[r]` vs `sell[r−1]`, `sell[r]` vs `buy[r+1]`; an empty opposite counts as 0, the own side must reach the minimum, in whole quantity units) and are marked by shape and position (an underline under the imbalanced side's number, never at the column's left edge where the vertical open/close strip is), not color alone. Volume without an aggressor side is never dropped silently: a cell of only such volume prints `?N` (never `0 | 0`) and the legend counts it. `FootprintFrame::build` checks the trades' own row extent against its buffer before `ProfileRows::build`, so it never grows it. **A drawn cell always carries its numbers, whole**: `prepare` keeps a bar as cells only if its whole column is on the plot, every number fits its room (`numbers_fit`, from the font's measured tabular digit widths; quantities print through `QtyFormat`, so crypto decimals count), rows are at least `MIN_TEXT_ROW_PX` tall, and no cell lies under the readout/legend block (`set_legend_block`, from the last render); otherwise the bar is its candle and `Fallbacks` says why in the legend (the chart re-renders when they change). Never a shaded cell without its number (it would read as nothing traded). Auto-fit raises the range's top clear of that block (`clear_top`). Horizontal price/crosshair lines break across drawn cells (`hline_segments`). Tested with Kraken 8-decimal quantities and ES 1h/1D volumes (`check_drawable`).

- Local store (`lines-feed/src/disk`, decision 0019): per contract `store-v1/<source>/<contract>/<trade date>.lseg`, append-only **segment v1** (v2 only for an equity or option contract, as `.lfr` v4; header like `.lfr` v3/v4 + source, encoding, trade date; blocks `u32 len | u32 crc32 | i64 time_us | lines-events-1`, one block per feed frame). Opening a segment for writing truncates at the first invalid block (torn tail; never repaired); readers stop at an incomplete block without consuming it, so they run alongside the writer. One `ContractWriter` per contract, owned by the feed (`EventSink` in `connection.rs`), exclusive via `writer.lock`; file I/O on its own thread. Flush policy: write when 256 KiB are buffered or every 200 ms, `sync_data` at most every 1 s and on close (crash loses ≤ 200 ms, power loss ≤ ~1.2 s). Stored: trades (with aggressor) and best bid/ask; depth only for `--store-depth` products; never option quotes (a chain is a live snapshot: after a restart it comes from the provider again). Not stored: provider history (it lives in the bar index), so a stored session replays its *trades*.
- Bar index (`bars`, CRC'd, replaced atomically) is a cache of the chart's bars, saved every 30 s and at each disconnect; rebuilt from segments if missing or damaged. Startup: the feed thread loads it into the chart store before connecting (panes show it at once), then requests history from the earliest hole in the bar coverage inside the window (else the newest bar); `lines_core::stitch` merges without double counting.
- Stored order flow (`disk::orderflow`, `lib.rs::warm_start`): after the bar cache loads and before connecting, the feed thread replays the stored trades of the newest `FeedConfig::orderflow_sessions` stored trade dates (default 2, 0 = off) in one pass over the segments into the tape, VAP, profiles and tick-bar builders (`ChartData::apply_stored`: never the bars, which already hold them; `counted_n` counts them with the live ones; `stored` keeps what was replayed, so a tick-bar rebuild reads exactly those trades again) and the indicator engine. Only trades inside the trade coverage count, and the segments hold exactly the trades live counted: `ChartData::apply` records per frame which trades it counted (`frame_counted`) and the writer, right after on the feed thread, stores only those (a reconnect's re-sent trades a snapshot covers are not stored; a connection's late trades, a REST fill after newer streamed ones, are), so a stored session replays to what live built; bars rebuilt from segments are claimed complete only where trades are; a corrupt or torn segment withdraws its coverage after the last good block; a segment evicted meanwhile is skipped. `stored_through` (store and engine) is the newest replayed trade's exchange time (never the coverage end, which follows the local clock) and drops a live trade at or before it, so a history snapshot older than the store never counts a trade twice. Panes show bars first and the header says `loading order flow…` (`ChartData::orderflow_loading`); trades go in 20k slices so the store lock is held for milliseconds. Replays and screenshots use no store unless `--store-dir` names one.
- Coverage (`coverage`, text): `bars` and `trades` ranges known complete. Claimed only after `HistoryComplete`: bars from the requested history start, trades from the newest snapshot `as_of` + 1 ms, both to the newest of feed time and trade time. A `Gap` closes the claim; a `HistoryGap` is excluded; a disconnect ends it. Trade claims count only where the segment file still exists (eviction) and stop at the last valid block after a torn-tail cut. Coinbase reports history beyond its page budget as a `HistoryGap`.
- Partition = session trade date via `disk::partition::trade_date` (from `catalog::sessions`; data outside every session goes to the next session's date; products without a calendar use the UTC date).
- Retention: oldest trade dates first across all contracts until segments fit the budget (default 4 GiB); segments being written are never deleted.
- Stale (docs/design.md §7): `ChartStore::stale_for()` (lock-free) is `Some` while the feed is `Live`, its market open, and nothing arrived for longer than `stale_after` (5 s; CQG 10 s; `FeedConfig::stale_after` overrides). Panes poll it with their revision, show a `STALE · no data N s` badge, and the DOM and T&S dim. Market-open comes from the product's sessions calendar, evaluated at the feed's clock (last market time + silence), so a closed market is never stale and replays stay deterministic.
- Latency HUD (roadmap 6b; `lines_core::latency`, app `latency_hud.rs`): off by default (`Workspace::show_latency_hud`, optional saved field), mirrored in the process-wide `latency::enabled()`; while it is off nothing records (one relaxed load per feed frame and per paint), a chart drops its receive → paint window and a feed's `ChartData::latency` is freed on its next frame; the trading core's two order windows (~62 KB) exist from its start and keep their samples across a toggle (`tests/latency_hud.rs` in lines-feed and lines-trading). Windows are 60 one-second histograms of quarter-octave buckets, keyed by the caller's clock in ms: feed exchange → receive on the frame's receive time (a replay's recorded time, so replays and screenshots are deterministic), order click → sent and the ack round trip (first ack of a `Submitted` order, `at − created`) on the trading core's clock, receive → paint on the monotonic clock from the last update that moved `chart_revision` (depth below the touch doesn't reset it), recorded only for frames that draw the data; while the pane is hidden its poll marks updates seen (`Pane::on_hidden_tick`), so a hidden tab's wait is not paint latency. The order windows read at the newer of the core's clock and the chart's feed clock, so they age out while the core idles. Back-fill is not latency: HTTP frames and frames a session flags with `ProviderSession::backfill_frame` (Kraken's trade snapshots) are left out, and so is any trade more than 60 s late; a session that streams past trades must flag them. Every lock is copied from and dropped before text is built; the trading core and the UI only `try_lock` the order windows (decision 0012). A quantile is its bucket's upper edge (never below the true value, at most 25% above). Feed and ack are whole milliseconds (`<1` below that); a feed whose samples are all exactly 0 with a clock-offset bound of exactly 0 has no separate exchange time and reads `n/a` (a clock behind the exchange also records 0s, but a negative bound). The clock line is `ClockOffset`'s bound `min(receive − exchange)` for wall-clock (live) feeds only, with a ⚠ badge (text, not color alone) beyond ±250 ms. Recording and reading never allocate (`no_alloc.rs`); the header text is rebuilt at render at most once a second, and the screenshot path switches recording on before it replays. A frozen frame leaves out the wall-clock measures (paint, order).
- Trading core (`lines-trading`, roadmap 6; decisions 0004/0005/0006/0012/0018): every order action is a `Command` in an `Envelope` (id, `Source`, the input event's `Instant`) through `TradingHandle::submit`; each gets exactly one `Event::Command` result (`Ok(order ids)` or a `Reject` with a `RejectCode` and message). Everything else comes out as `Event`s on `TradingHandle::subscribe` (Order, Fill, Position, Account, Breach, Guardrail, Strategy, Rebuilt), in order. `TradingCore` is pure (time passed in, no I/O) and runs on the `TradingService` thread; it never waits on a lock the UI holds: the only lock it shares is the latency HUD's `OrderLatency`, which both sides only `try_lock` (a sample that meets the UI reading is dropped). Order states move only along `OrderState::can_become` (Submitted → Acked → Working → PartiallyFilled → Filled / Cancelled / Rejected with a reason); nothing is Working before its ack. Money is exact: `Money(i128)` in 1e-10 currency units, `ContractSpec::money_per_unit` must divide exactly; positions are FIFO lots, so realised P&L is exact. Risk (`RiskLimits`: max order qty, max position counting open orders, price band in ticks from the last trade else the mid, daily loss) and prop rules run before the broker; `CancelOrder`, `CancelAll` and `Flatten` never pass through them. Open orders (`open_qty`) include live bracket exits and count an OCO pair once; only exits held for a working entry with nothing live (never activated, or their sibling closed what was open) are left out, and the strategy gate adds those held exits on top. So a manual close of a bracketed position counts its live exits as already closing: in a guardrail cooldown or reducing only it is checked as an entry (Flatten stays the way out). A `ModifyOrder` that adds quantity is a new order for the increase (guardrails, trading state, contract cap, risk, submit rate) and the whole order meets the order-size limit; a moved price meets the band, a stop's trigger too. Prop rules are data (`RulePreset`, labelled "user-editable approximation", no firm names): trailing drawdown intraday (equity high-water) or end-of-day (closing balances), optional lock at start + X, daily loss per trade date, micro-equivalent contract cap (`Product::micro_equivalent`), profit target, consistency; a breach blocks new orders and, with `liquidate_on_breach`, flattens (`Source::Rule`). Day boundaries are session trade dates (`disk::partition::trade_date`) of the account's `day_product`. Reconnect/restart: `rebuild` replaces balances, positions and open orders from `Broker::snapshot` (a contract it no longer lists is reported flat); orders the broker doesn't know become Cancelled. Accounts a broker discovers after connecting join with `add_accounts` (`TradingHandle::add_accounts`), which rebuilds. `BrokerEvent::ExternalFill` (an execution on an order Lines didn't place) becomes an `Event::Fill` with order `EXTERNAL_ORDER` (0) and no realised P&L for the journal, but never moves positions or balances: the broker's snapshot already holds them, so nothing is counted twice; repeats are dropped by `trade_id`. A broker that reports them says so (`BrokerCaps::external_fills`); for it, `rebuild` first applies what the broker has queued (fills before the positions they made), and adds no reconciled fill for orders adopted from a snapshot (their executions already came as external fills). External fills for an account not added yet are held (10k) and applied when it joins. Such a broker lists an account only once its positions are known and from then on reports every execution on it once, including those made while disconnected (after the reconnect), and none from before it was listed. Every request reaches the broker through `Broker::request_in_context` with a `RequestContext`: `source`, `origin` (`Origin::of`: `Rule`/`Api`/`Strategy` automated, the rest manual) and the issuing `PaneContext` (pane contract, `ContractSelection` follows-product or pinned, the resolver's `front_month`), set by `submit_from_pane` (`TradingHandle`/`TradingCore`); plain `submit` sends `pane: None`. The default implementation calls `request`, so brokers that don't need it (paper) ignore it. Latency: click → `Broker::request` is logged as `tracing` target `lines::latency` (`click_to_sent_us`) and kept in a 4096-sample ring (`TradingHandle::latency`).
- Strategies (decision 0025; `lines-trading/src/strategy.rs`, `core/strategy_gate.rs`, `lines-strategy`): a strategy only acts through commands with `Source::Strategy(id)` (`Origin::Automated`), and the **core** enforces the rules. Each strategy command passes the strategy gate first, then prop rules, risk and guardrails as usual. The gate checks four things:
  - **State** (Disarmed / Armed / Paused / Stopped): only Armed may enter (a bracket, an order beyond what closes the position, a modify adding quantity). Every state may reduce: cancel its own orders, flatten its own contract, move its exits (a modify of an entry order, price included, is an entry), and place orders no larger than the position less what open orders and held bracket exits (each bracket once) already close. A strategy's Cancel All cancels only its own orders; its Flatten does too when there is nothing to close, but closing a position cancels every order on that contract (a trader's stop left after the position is gone would open a new one). Repricing or resizing an entry is an entry; whether a single order is closing counts the other open orders and held exits on its side. **Exits skip every check below** (hours, max position, daily loss, rate).
  - **Scope**: the instance's own account, contract and orders, with at most one instance per account and contract.
  - **Limits** (`StrategyLimits`, all required): max position (worst case with open orders), max daily loss on its own fills (its own orders, plus anyone's Flatten fills up to what closes its own position), entries per 60 s (counted only when the core accepts them), and entry hours in a named time zone.
  - **Paper only**: `Broker::simulated()` is true only for the paper broker; live brokers and a venue's own Sim accounts keep `false`.
  - Who issues controls: only a person (`Origin::Manual`) arms. A strategy may pause or stop only itself, and may never add, remove or arm.
  - `StopStrategies` is never gated and acts at once. Every instance stops before any flatten.
  - **The core pauses** an Armed instance for any of these: a manual order action on its account and contract (any contract for account-wide Cancel All or Flatten; before the action runs, even if it is then refused; the order the person modifies or cancels is left to them); the account not Active, or a guardrail lockout; the broker disconnected; its daily loss reached. The trading service pauses every armed instance when the strategy host, having sent a heartbeat, sends none for `HEARTBEAT_TIMEOUT` (5 s) of **wall-clock** time (decision 0027: a fast replay moves the market clock minutes in a moment).
  - **Daily loss (decision 0027):** reaching it **flattens** the instance's account and contract (every order there cancelled), once per trade date, also when the host had paused it with a trade open; never after Stop or when a person's own order paused it. Arming is refused until the next trade date.
  - Commands the core issues itself (a pause's cancels, Stop with flatten, a rule's liquidation, the daily-loss flatten) take ids from `INTERNAL_COMMAND_IDS` (2^62) up, never an id a `TradingHandle` gave out.
  - The strategy host (`lines_strategy::host`) runs instances on its own thread. It feeds them their feed's batches (`listen`, attached before anything else), their level sources (`LevelFeed`, polled by revision), their core events (`concerns`, the one definition shared with backtests) and a timer, and sends their commands as `Source::Strategy(id)`. Its own pauses: a stale feed, missing or stale levels, a contract change, a panic (the instance is then never called again). It adds instances Disarmed and never arms.
  - Pausing or stopping cancels entry orders (`Role::Entry`, and singles that don't only close the position). Bracket exits and Flatten orders stay.
  - Nothing re-arms automatically. Armed state is never saved. Arming reconciles the instance's own books with the account (flat adopts flat; any other mismatch is refused: flatten first). Removing an instance disowns its remaining orders. An instance owns only the orders its own Place, Bracket and Flatten created, never orders its Cancel All or Flatten cancelled.
  - Strategies are pure (`lines_strategy::Strategy`: no I/O, no clock, `Ctx::now` passed in).
  - The strategy panel (`lines-app/src/strategy_panel.rs` pure, `strategy_view.rs`) reads instances through `StrategySource` (the host, or `DemoStrategies` in tests and `--strategies-demo` screenshots) and each state from the core's `Event::Strategy` (`TradeState::strategy`) when it has one: the core enforces it. HALTED is exactly `strategy::DAILY_LOSS`. Its Start, Stop and Stop all go straight to the core through `Trading::submit` (a manual source), never through the host, so they work if the host hangs; Stop and Stop all flatten (decision 0025's default; "Stop all, keep positions" is palette-only). Stop all is `commands::BINDINGS` Ctrl+Shift+R from any pane, drawn with `popover::always_live`, and strategy commands never key-repeat. Start has no key. The DOM's trading switch counts the instances armed on its account (`TradeState::strategies_armed`, from the account `Event::Strategy` carries) because it doesn't stop them: `trade_bar::switch_text_armed` keeps the state and the whole count at every width (`Off · 2 strat` narrow, `Disabled · 2 strat`, `Disabled · 2 strategies armed` from `SWITCH_STRATEGIES`, with `Trading:` from `SWITCH_WORDS`), with a count the switch doesn't shrink (the account chip gives way), a reason orders would be denied moves to the status line (`reason_on_status`), and a disabled panel's line leads with the count (`locked_hint`: `2 strategies still armed · DOM locked`; laid out as whole items, so the count is never cut), with "still" only when the switch is the only lock: another blocker may bind them too, and the core decides.
  - Chart strategy markers (`lines-app/src/strategy_marks.rs`): a strategy's entries and exits are fills (`TradeState`'s ring of the core's `Event::Fill`s, newest 4096) of the orders the core counts as its own (`strategy_owns`: what its Place, Bracket and Exit decisions created, its Flatten's own orders, their bracket exits; never an order a decision only cancelled), classed by replaying **every** fill on the account and contract, so a flatten it didn't decide keeps the next ones right (a fill that grows the position is an entry, a reversing one both an exit and an entry); skipped signals are decisions that created no orders (no command, refused, or never sent), at their first level's price. A mark sits on the bar holding its time (never part-way to the next). Shapes, not color alone, on a background halo: wedges for entries (up under a buy, down over a sell), an X for an exit, a hollow circle for a skip; instance, time and reason show on hover. Marks are rebuilt in the chart's poll when the source's revision, the fill count or the contract moves; prepare and paint don't allocate (positions go into a buffer reserved at rebuild). Per chart, saved as the optional `ChartState.hide_strategy_marks`; `Command::Strategy(ToggleMarks)` (palette, the chart's pane menu; a popped-out chart runs it too). Hiding studies or Clean chart leaves them shown, as drawings.
  - Backtests (`lines_strategy::backtest::run`) are single-threaded. Market frames go through the recording's own provider session (`lines_feed::recorded::RecordedEvents`, the same decode as replay); levels come from `read_recording` and `LevelSource::decode`. At one instant: levels, then market, then timer. The instance is armed once data started and every required source is current, and is never re-armed.
  - The same inputs give byte-identical `Report::to_json` (tested). Reports carry file names and FNV-1a hashes, never local paths.
- Guardrails (`lines-trading/src/guardrails.rs`, roadmap 8b, decision 0006): per account, all optional: max trades per trade date (a round trip flat → position → flat; partial fills don't count, a reversal closes one and opens the next), a losing streak (trade P&L net of every fee; below zero is a loss) → cooldown of N minutes, max daily loss in money (realised + unrealised, separate from prop rules), max position (worst case with open orders), no entries in the last N minutes before the session close, and a news blackout (below). A breach is a lockout (`Cooldown` until a time, `Locked` until the trade date ends) with a reason; denials are `RejectCode::Guardrail` with a short US-English message ("Cooldown: 3 losses in a row, 12 min left"). Exits are never blocked: Flatten, Cancel All, cancels and orders no larger than what is left to close; a modify that adds quantity is checked as an entry. `Command::SetGuardrails`: tighter fields apply at once, looser ones with the next trade date; nothing clears a lockout early. Trade dates from `disk::partition::trade_date` of the account's `day_product`; a fill counts toward the trade date of its own timestamp (the broker's fill time), not the time the core processes it. `Event::Guardrail { account, state, reason, until }` on every change, its end included (the core wakes for it). Saved per account in the trading file (`SavedAccount.guardrails`, format v2); a broker venue's accounts (no trading file) in `<config dir>/paper/<extension name>.guardrails.json` whatever the data source, replays included (`TradingSetup::venue_dir`; `persist::GuardFile`, its own version 1, per account the tracker plus the trade date and its opening balance, so today's loss survives a restart; a newer or unreadable file is set aside and reported, and the run starts from settings), written by the venue's service (`TradingService::start_venue`) at once on a lockout or its end, else at most once a second, and holding saved entries of accounts not joined yet; `TradingCore::set_join_guardrails` restores each when the broker lists it (`add_accounts`), then asks for its settings like `SetGuardrails`, and reports a restored lockout. Defaults: `settings.json` `guardrails` keyed by account name (or id), asked for at every start and edited in Settings › Risk (each change is submitted as `SetGuardrails`; a loosening asks first and says it takes effect at the next trade date). Prop rules change the same way (`Command::SetRules`, `RulePreset::tightest`): what is tighter applies at once and checks the next order (orders already sent keep the rules they were checked against), what is looser waits for the next trade date, during a breach too; the account size is the account's starting balance; a tighter rule the account is already past (today's loss beyond the new daily limit, equity beyond the new drawdown, where the drawdown is the whole rule: amount, mode and lock, since a higher lock moves a locked threshold, more contracts open or working than the new cap) is refused with the reason unless that rule is already breached, so a setting never breaches, liquidates or blocks the account's own exits; `SetRules` publishes the account's view without checking breaches (the next equity update does, with the real balance); `SetGuardrails` checks today's loss against a tighter daily loss at once (a lock, which never liquidates nor blocks exits); at startup settings are sent only after the rebuild has restored the real balance, on a clock other than the wall's (a replay) both startup rebuilds and the settings run at `TradingCore::BEFORE_DATA` (`UtcMillis(0)`, what the service's market clock reads before data), and nothing at or before it rolls a trade date: rolling at the wall's date, or 1970's, would make the first data's date a new one and bring in waiting rules early, and refusals are reported like a setting that doesn't resolve; a new trade date closes under the old rules before waiting ones take over; never from a strategy. Looser rules still waiting are saved with the account (`SavedAccount.pending_rules`, optional). The account's view (`AccountView.limits`, optional) carries the rules and guardrails in force and those waiting, so the UI shows both. `settings.json` `prop_rules` (optional, keyed by account name, else id): a preset by name plus overrides of single values (`PropRulesDoc`), sent at every start; one that doesn't resolve is reported and left out. Screenshot scripts: `guard trades N | streak L/M | loss X | position N | close M | news warn|block|flatten`.

- News blackout (`lines-trading/src/news.rs`, roadmap 8b): `NewsBlackout` (action warn / block / flatten, `before_min`/`after_min` default 2, high importance unless `include_medium`) is an optional field on `Guardrails` and on `RulePreset`; the core uses the tighter of each part (`TradingCore::news_rule`), and tighten-now / loosen-tomorrow applies to it like every guardrail. It is checked in the core's guardrail path, on the core's clock (market clock in replays), never in the UI. Exits always pass (Flatten, Cancel All, cancels, orders that only close). The window is `[release − before, release + after)` on the products the release `affects`. Flatten: the core wakes at each window start (`next_wake`) and flattens each affected position once per window with a `Source::Rule` Flatten; entries stay refused for the window. A blackout is not a lockout: it raises no `Event::Guardrail` and no `GuardState` change (the UI reads the same schedule, `econ_ui::schedule()`, and the rule in force from `AccountView.limits`, which is what `TradingCore::news_rule` decides from). A flatten the core refuses is retried on later ticks in the window. A calendar that can't answer is never clear: no schedule, a date from `now − after` to `now + before` outside the coverage of a release the rule counts (at least its importance; Missing), or a `version` more than `STALE_AFTER_DAYS` (60) old (Stale) refuses entries under block and flatten (warn refuses nothing) but flattens nothing. Only new entries and quantity increases are checked: an entry order resting from before the window may fill in it, and moving its price is not checked (flatten closes such a fill on the next tick; block doesn't). The schedule (`lines_core::econ`, bundled `lines-feed/data/econ-calendar.txt`, format 1) holds official sources only (BLS, BEA, Federal Reserve), times in America/New_York converted with the bundled tzdb; a local time in a DST gap or fold is refused at parse; every line says `verified` or `unverified`. UI (phase 2): every reader goes through `econ_ui::schedule()` (the bundled schedule, or a screenshot's `--econ-calendar`), and the paper and venue cores are given the same one, so a chart, the trade panel and the core never disagree. The rule shown is the core's in force (`AccountView.limits`, the tighter of the guardrail's and the prop preset's, `econ_ui::rule_of`). The trade panel's status line shows a release within 30 minutes that moves the pane's product (`Scheduled release: CPI in 14:32`), a window in force as a strip with the time left, and a calendar that can't answer under Block or Flatten; while either refuses entries, the switch says why (`news blackout`; `calendar stale`, or `no calendar` when it doesn't cover the date or none is loaded) and entries dim (exits, Flatten and Cancel all never do). Charts mark each release that moves their product (medium and up) with a dashed line and a tag at the foot of the plot (`econ_layer::EconLayer`), up to the plot's right edge (a release ahead of the last bar shows in the margin): high filled in `warn` with `on_warn` text, medium outlined; tags that would overlap stack up to three rows, past which the line alone marks a release. Settings › Risk edits the rule per account (`guard.news`, `guard.news_before`, `guard.news_after`, `guard.news_medium`) like the other guardrails: tighter now, looser at the next session.
- Paper broker (`paper.rs`): deterministic, I/O-free, driven by `market` (feed batches via `lines_feed::listen` and `FeedBridge`) and `poll(now)`. Acks after `latency_ms` (default 25). Rejects off-tick prices, non-positive qty and orders while the product's session is closed. Fill models for resting limits: trade-through (default: a trade strictly through the limit), touch (a trade at the limit), queue estimate (behind the book's size at the price; trades at the price and a shrinking level reduce the queue). Several orders never fill more than a trade printed. Market orders and marketable limits walk the book; liquidity taken isn't reused until that level updates; no book → last trade; neither → reject. Stops trigger on trades at or through the trigger. Fees per quantity unit per side, by product. Brackets and OCO are held broker-side (exits Acked until the entry fills, sized to the fill; an exit fill cancels or shrinks the sibling) and saved in the broker state, so they survive restarts.
- Trade journal (`lines-trading/src/journal`, roadmap 8c): a trade is one round trip per account + contract, flat → position → flat, by `guardrails::RoundTrip` (the one definition, shared with the guardrails' counts); a reversal closes one trade and opens the next, its fill's quantity split and its fee split pro rata (remainder to the closing side). Realized P&L comes from the journal's own FIFO lots (exact; right for reconciled fills too), fees from the fills. Fills are keyed by `trade_id` within a run: a fill seen again (reconciled twice, delivered twice) never counts twice; never across runs (paper fill ids restart when the paper file is reset, and the core never re-emits an earlier run's fills). Trade ids are unique (`<first fill id>@<entry ms>`, `#n` on a collision in a file), so a new trade never replaces a saved one. MAE/MFE: the worst/best of realized-so-far + open P&L (before fees) while open, from the market batches the paper broker sees (trades and last prices) and the fills. Everywhere it is shown or exported, money is the whole position and a tick number is per contract; MAE/MFE ticks are `Trade::excursion_ticks` (those dollars at the largest size), never a price excursion from `low`/`high`, which include prices from before a later add. It runs on its own thread (`JournalShared`): the trading thread's `service::Observer` only sends it messages (market batches after the core; the core's events before each market batch and after each drain, so a trade opened earlier in a batch sees the prices after it), the UI reads published snapshots (an `Arc` pointer swap) and sends edits and exports as messages: no lock is shared with the core, none is held while formatting. Only a trade closing, the open count changing or an edit publishes; market data and the core's per-batch position reports never do (tested through the service). The file is opened append-only and re-read at open, so a torn tail is cut from the file as it is then, never a stale length. The app flushes it on quit. File `<config dir>/journal/<source>.jsonl` (live sources only; replays and screenshots in memory): JSON Lines, header `{"journal":2,"source":…}` (v2: contracts may be equities or options; a v1 file reads as it is, and opening it for writing keeps `<stem>.v1.jsonl` and swaps in the same records under a v2 header, atomically), then `{"trade":…}` / `{"edit":…}` records, externally tagged on purpose (serde's internal tagging buffers content and can't hold `i128` money); append-only on its own thread; a torn last line is dropped on read and cut before appending; a newer version is left alone and not written. Open trades aren't saved: after a restart a position the journal didn't see open is seeded from the core's position report, marked `incomplete` and dated from the first fill it sees. The Journal pane follows `pnl_display` (off: no money or tick number in rows, the stats strip or the by-account and by-tag tables, whose lines are then ordered by name so the order doesn't tell the P&L either; those tables are money only, so in ticks mode too they show counts and win rates and order by name; CSV export is always complete) and formats rows only when the journal's revision, the display setting, the account list, the trade date, the date range or the group moved. Untagged trades group under an empty key, which no tag can be. - OMS checklist (docs/inspiration.md, one test each in `tests/trading.rs`, `checklist_*`): a command `Reject` is always a **denial** (the core refused, nothing sent); a broker's refusal is `OrderState::Rejected` on a sent order, and the UI labels them DENIED / REJECTED. Risk adds `max_notional`, `max_submits_per_sec` and `max_modifies_per_sec` (separate windows); cancels and Flatten are never rate limited. Brackets are one-updates-other: an exit's remaining is the bracket's open quantity (entry fills grow both exits, an exit fill shrinks its sibling); with nothing open the sibling is held while the entry still works and cancelled once it is done. - Position brackets (`core/position_bracket.rs`, `Command::SetBracketMode`, owner request): per account and contract, manual trades get exits per entry (default) or as **one stop and one target for the whole position** (`BracketMode::Position { stop_ticks, target_ticks }`).
  - **Opening:** when the position opens, the pair is placed through `PlaceExit` (reduce-only, OCO), at the offsets from the exact average fill price. Each price is rounded onto the tick grid toward the entry, so neither is ever further than its offset.
  - **Never at once:** a leg that would fill or trigger at once against the core's last bid, ask or trade is not sent. Switching the mode on for such a position is refused.
  - **Following the position:** every sync (after each command, tick and market batch) compares each leg's remaining quantity with the position and resizes it, keeping its price (a person may move it).
    - Resizes go straight to the broker (`BrokerRequest::Modify`), with no guardrail, rate or reduce-only-growth check: they are protection.
    - **The broker must cap a reduce-only order at the position**, as the paper broker does. This is required of any broker with `oco`.
    - A resize still unanswered is sent again a second later, three times in all, and then the miss is published once. A resize that races a fill settles at the position.
  - **Flat and flips:** flat cancels the pair. A flip cancels it, and the new side gets its own pair once those cancels are confirmed: a new pair is placed only while no other exit works on the contract, so exits never add up past the position.
  - **Legs that end:** a leg that ends while the position is still open is placed again, at its last price (a person's move kept) unless the market is past it:
    - filled (flat and reopened within one poll, or an add left over): the new position is placed at the offsets from its own average, and the count of tries starts again;
    - ended by the broker: placed again;
    - `Unresolved` (a reconnect without it): never assumed gone, never placed again;
    - while a Flatten's order is open on the contract, nothing is placed. The Flatten's order is never paired with an exit as OCO.
  - **Refused legs:** a placement the core or the broker refuses is tried again a second later, three tries per leg, each refusal published. Out of tries, it says so once. A new pair waiting more than 5 s for other exits to finish also says so.
  - **A person's cancel:** a leg a person cancels (× or Cancel All, recorded from the command) stays cancelled until the position is flat, and so does a leg whose kind they place an exit for while it is down. Exits they place are their own: never resized or adopted, so a scale-out order is never grown.
  - **Entries and second exits:** in this mode a person's bracket order loses its exits (sent as the entry alone), and a person's exit of a kind whose leg is working is refused.
    - Brackets from automated sources other than strategies (`Source::Api`) keep their exits. Their exits and the pair are both reduce-only, so the broker's cap applies to their total.
  - **Switching to position mode:**
    - existing exits that already are one stop and one target, each for the whole position on the closing side, are adopted as the pair, as after a restart (the app sets the saved mode again); a missing one of the two is placed;
    - any other working exits are refused unless `replace`, with the count in the message (`exits_replaced_by_position_bracket`, what the confirmation names); with `replace` they are cancelled first.
  - **Back to per entry:** the pair stays, as two ordinary exits.
  - **Strategies:** the mode is refused, and does nothing, while a strategy instance trades the account and contract, and a strategy can't be added while the mode is on. A strategy's exits are its own.
  - **App** (`trade_bar.rs`, `brackets.rs`): the trade panel's Bracket row has a **Pos** toggle (palette: "Trade: bracket for the whole position / per entry").
    - The setting is saved per account and product symbol, as `bracket_presets.<account>.position` (optional; absent = per entry). It is saved only once the core accepts the switch.
    - A pane tells the core its saved mode (with its preset, or the first one, which becomes the pane's) when it first draws, and again after a new preset or another account or contract. The core adopts the pair that is already there.
    - The command is routed like an order: to the account's core, refused on a read-only account. Its note is silent, so a refusal isn't a DENIED flash; the panel answers it.
    - A refusal naming the exits switching would cancel becomes the panel's confirmation, which takes the editor's place. The confirmation keeps its account and contract and is dropped when the pane moves; "Replace N exits" sends `replace` for exactly those.
    - While Pos is saved on, any refusal (or no preset to take its distances from) marks the cell "Pos !" in the warning color: Pos isn't in force, and a click turns it off (per entry, which always works). Turning it on again retries. Render treats a busy store as unknown, never as "off", so nothing is sent on it. The entries then carry the pane's preset. A refusal while turning it on from off is only the person's flash.
    - A pane that told the core Position for its account and contract tells it per entry once Pos is turned off (by any pane). An answer never overwrites a preset picked meanwhile. Clicks read the setting with a blocking read; render only tries.
    - Answers are read with `try_read`, never blocking a frame. A command unanswered for 10 s counts as lost.
    - The switch acts from a DOM's panel only (`chart_runs` excludes it). Off stays clickable while Pos is on and says why it can't be chosen; the bracket key skips it.

Esc, the button again, hiding the strip (or a plot too narrow for its button) or another symbol drops the pick, so a price never carries to another product.
- Settings (decision 0021, roadmap 6c; `settings_schema.rs` pure, `settings_view.rs` the window): one table of `Setting`s (key, section, name, description, scope, safety class, kind = validation, get/set on `Prefs`, default) drives the Settings window's rows and search, the palette (`Command::Setting`: a choice or toggle sets that value; anything else opens the window at it; settings that already have palette commands, such as P&L display, the hint bar and the latency HUD, add none) and storage; no setting has a hand-built form. Scope is shown on every row (App, Workspace, New panes, Account, Account · product, Read-only). Changes apply at once through one path (`settings_view::commit`: write, then effects: the look via `workspace::set_theme`, P&L text, workspace preferences, guardrails to the core); Ctrl+Z undoes, ↺ / Ctrl+R resets. Trading-affecting settings carry the TRADING mark. A change that loosens trading risk (`settings_schema::loosening`: a guardrail made looser or removed, an opt-in "disable trading" turned off, a larger default quantity) asks first, from the window, the palette, a reset or an undo, and the confirmation is shown for the account it applies to; a guardrail loosening takes effect at the next trade date, the others at once; a guardrail tightening applies now (the core's rule, unchanged). The core is sent the account's guardrails from the entry that was edited (name, else id, as startup reads them). Numbers are validated exactly (out of range is refused, never clamped). New `settings.json` fields are optional (`appearance`, `new_panes`) and not written at their defaults; keys a newer version wrote are kept (`Settings::unknown`, flattened) so saving never drops them. A look flag (`--theme`, `--palette`, `--hollow`) holds over Settings › Appearance for its run (`PaneEnv::look_flags`, shown in the window) until the trader sets that field, and is never saved: a change saves only the fields it changed; `--dom-grouping`/`--dom-trail` win for new DOMs for their run; a loosening is confirmed with Y, not Enter; screenshots use defaults (they read no user files). Ctrl+, opens the one window (or brings it forward). Sections: Appearance, Charts, DOM, Trading, Risk (guardrails, prop rules, lockout and breach states), Accounts, Data, Keys, Diagnostics; Risk rows read what the core reports each frame (in force, waiting, locked out; a busy cache keeps the last frame's); a shown window repaints when an account's revision moves, so it never shows a stale state while idle. A risk change the core refuses puts back the entries it wrote (unless rewritten since) and says why on the row.
- P&L display (`pnl.rs`, owner request): `settings.json` `pnl_display` = off | ticks | currency | both (default both; optional field, not written at the default), app-wide; `Command::CyclePnl` (Ctrl+Alt+P) and `Command::SetPnl` (palette, DOM pane menu). Shown: the DOM header's open P&L for the pane's contract (ticks one decimal from the average price; currency exact from `Money`), the account's day P&L (currency modes only), the chart's position line (dashed, at the average price) with a tag. Always signed and in the up/down colour, never colour alone. Off: no money or tick number anywhere in the trading UI (side, quantity and average price stay; the rule strip shows states without amounts, also in ticks mode; refusal messages lose their amounts via `pnl::redact_amounts`). Panes repaint on a change through `pnl::display_epoch`. Screenshots: `--pnl MODE`. Chart fill marks wait for chart trading (roadmap 8).
- Pane headers keep a right-hand gutter (`pane::controls_gutter`, sized for the most icons `pane::controls` gives any header, and tested) where the hover controls draw, in main windows and pane windows; header content sits in `pane::header_content`, clipped before it, so hover chrome never covers anything (the chart's account chip sits with the contract on the left). The controls and the tab close are icon buttons (`icons::button`: drawn in code, 20 px hits, a tooltip with the key after 400 ms, the button role and the action's name for assistive technology); every header control's name comes from `pane::controls`, never from an icon alone.
- Tests never assert on what a real-time feed did within a fixed wall-clock time (other builds load the CPU): they run until the condition holds with a generous deadline (`tests/feed.rs` `run_until`), assert on what was delivered, or test the decision with the clock injected (`ChartStore::stale_after_quiet`). Throughput and latency budgets live only in `#[ignore]`d perf tests.
- The UI never blocks on the feed. Sessions decode outside the lock, the store applies events under a short lock, the UI polls an atomic revision every 8 ms and `try_lock`s in paint.
- Transport `recv` must be cancel-safe.
- macOS always builds `gpui_platform` with `font-kit` (target-specific dependency in `lines-app`); without it GPUI's no-op text system renders no text. `lines-app/tests/text_system.rs` (no harness, main thread) asserts the platform text system lists fonts and shapes glyphs.
- Screenshot mode exits from inside the app with its own code (quitting the app exits 0 on macOS); every failure exits non-zero with no file.
- Screenshot mode: repeated runs with the same inputs on one machine produced byte-identical PNGs. Output dimensions are checked against `size × scale`.
- Dropdowns (`lines-app/src/dropdown.rs`): a list opened from a chip (timeframe, account) opens directly under it: left edge on the chip's left edge, top 2 px under its bottom, in window coordinates (drawn deferred at `dropdown::PRIORITY`, so it is not clipped by the header or the pane and takes the pointer from what it covers; placed from the chip's visible part, nothing for a chip clipped away). It moves only when it would leave the window: left until it ends 4 px inside the right edge, or up above the chip when there is no room below (`dropdown::place`, tested). The window's own overlays (pane/tab menus, palette and prompts, the switch-all banner) are deferred at `dropdown::OVERLAY_PRIORITY`, above any open list; a new window-level overlay must be too. Never position a chip's list with fixed offsets in the pane body.
- Popovers (`lines-app/src/popover.rs`: the studies menu, the timeframe and account lists, pane/tab/column/detached-pane menus): a press outside an open popover closes it and does nothing else (no pan, drawing select, order or focus change under it): each draws a transparent, occluding, window-wide backdrop just under itself (`popover::defer_backdrop`, deferred at `dropdown::BACKDROP_PRIORITY`, below every list and menu) whose handler applies `popover::outside_click` and stops the press; a press on its own chip just closes it. The exception: Flatten and Cancel all stay one press (docs/design.md §3). `popover::always_live` draws them deferred over every backdrop (`dropdown::BACKDROP_PRIORITY` < `LIVE_PRIORITY` < `PRIORITY` (lists, studies menu) < `OVERLAY_PRIORITY` (menus, palette)), clipped as where they sit in every phase, so a clipped button takes no press outside its pane (`popover::Masked`; GPUI masks a deferred draw's paint only); their own left-button handler takes the press and closes the popover (`popover::close_any`). Esc closes it (the studies menu steps back first: a template name, an editor, the filter; at its top Esc closes it). The release of a press that closed a popover does nothing either (`popover::mark_dismissing`; the chart finishes no drawing on it). Opening the symbol picker closes the chart's popovers. State not yet applied (the studies menu's open editor, cursor, filter, a template name half typed) stays as a draft the next open restores. One popover is open at a time in the app: every open goes through `popover::opened`, which closes the previous one (any pane or window) after the current update. A new popover = a `popover::Kind`, an `opened` call, and `dropdown(..).on_dismiss(..)` or `popover::layer`; pane-level `on_mouse_down` closes are only the fallback for a list whose chip is clipped away (it draws no backdrop).
- Colours are tokens in `crates/lines-app/src/theme.rs` only (a test rejects colour literals in views); user-chosen colors (decision 0026) are data, turned into colors only by `theme.rs`; the style editor's quick custom swatches (`CUSTOM_SWATCHES`) are clear of every color with a meaning and read as lines in all four combinations (tested). A study part's theme colors are the study colors (`Theme::study`, named by `study_names`); a part's own color (`Color::Own`) is the theme's or its source's and is never warned on, any other color is checked as a drawing's is. Themes derive from the brand palette (ink `#11141B`, violet `#8C7BFF`, paper `#ECEAF4`); violet is the accent and last-price tag, never up/down. Every text token meets WCAG AA (4.5:1) on its backgrounds in all four theme/palette combinations (`theme::tests`). Direction is shown by shape too: one direction's bodies are hollow (bodies under 3 device px each way stay filled).
