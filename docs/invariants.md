## Invariants (enforced by tests)

- Prices are vendor-neutral: `Price(i64)` is an integer count of the *catalog product's* price unit, whose size is an exact rational `PriceScale` defined once in `catalog.rs` (ES/NQ/MES/MNQ: 1/100, tick 25 units). Quantities are `Qty(i64)`, a count of `10^-decimals` of the base unit (futures: decimals 0). No core type is in any vendor's units.
- Each provider converts its wire values exactly at its edge. CQG converts `scaled_price` with `UnitConverter`, from `correct_price_scale` turned into an exact rational. Coinbase parses decimal text, and REST numbers via `RawValue`. Sierra converts `f32 / multiplier` with an f32-relative tolerance, and the result must be on the tick grid. Values that aren't exactly representable are counted (`MarketEvent::Rejected`, import report), never rounded. CQG metadata whose tick disagrees with the catalog is a fatal `BadMetadata`.
- Instruments (decisions 0013, 0028): a `ContractKey` is a product plus a `Delivery`: a futures month, spot (crypto: one perpetual contract), an equity's shares, or a listed option `{ expiry, strike, right }` on the *underlying's* product, the strike an exact `Price` in its price units. The multiplier (100), exercise style and option tick are the product's `OptionRules`, never in the key, so one OCC symbol is one key. `Product::lists` says whether a key's kind fits its product. Option money is premium units × multiplier (`ContractSpec`). OCC symbols (`lines_feed::occ`, the 21-character OSI form) are the industry's standard, derived from the key by rule like futures symbols, so they are public; a strike OCC can't write exactly has no symbol.
- Vendor symbols exist only inside `lines-feed` (the crate-private `catalog::roots` table and `src/{cqg,coinbase,kraken}`), `lines-databento` (Databento's raw symbols and schema names, decision 0033) and the dev-only `lines-sierra`. The UI sees `Product`, `ContractKey`, `Capabilities` and the provider id. Bid/ask display is capability-gated.
- `ProviderSession`s do no I/O. HTTP responses arrive as `FrameKind::Http` frames carrying `URL
body` (pre-URL recordings: body only), so recordings contain them and sessions interpret a response by its URL, not their own request plan. Recordings hold inbound frames whole and outbound frames as their time only (no data: a logon carries credentials; replay skips them and needs only their time, its start clock; older files hold their bytes), and `LogonParams`' `Debug` shows only whether a credential is set. Replay drives the same session code; its clock is the recorded frame time (the first recorded frame before any delivery). An interactive replay is paced by the replay bar's `ReplayControl` (`lines_feed::replay_control`), which is also the replay's only clock: the store's `market_clock`/`feed_clock`/staleness and the trading core's market clock (`TradingHandle::follow_replay`) read it, so nothing moves while it is paused and everything runs at its speed. Every seek ends paused at its target. A seek back is a rewind: the transport ends, `run_feed` clears the store (`ChartStore::rewind`, like a roll with nothing retired, clocks included) and the indicator tap, and delivers the file again up to the target, which must give exactly the data a replay cut there gives (`tests/feed.rs`); the core's clock goes back once per rewind (`TradingCore::replay_rewound`, run before any replayed data): the paper broker's waiting requests take effect, its working orders are canceled and its books dropped (`Broker::rewound`), the core's marks and trigger prints forget the later clock's times, so no replayed quote is refused as older, and news flattens done after the new time can act again; data still on its way from before the jump is dropped by the service until the feed's restart marker (an empty listen batch, `lines_feed::listen::restarted`, sent after every batch from before the jump and before any replayed one: no timestamps involved); the app cancels the triggers and says how many orders and triggers went. A finished controlled replay keeps its feed task waiting for a rewind. Screenshots get a control only with `--replay-bar`.
- Option chains (decision 0028, `lines_core::chain`): a provider streams one `MarketEvent::OptionQuote { key, quote }` per series on its underlying's feed; the store keeps them in `ChartData::chains` (per expiry, strikes ascending; a quote replaces the series' whole quote) with their own revision, never moving `chart_epoch` (charts don't repaint for chain quotes) nor counting as the feed's updates; at most 400 expiries and 2,000 strikes per expiry are kept (more are counted as rejected). Quote prices are exact; IV and delta are display-only fixed-point integers (hundredths of a percent, ten-thousandths), so quotes compare exactly and replays are deterministic. In `lines-events-1` an option quote is tag 5, on the recording's own product; a product without listed options rejects it (counted). The chain pane (`chain_view`, a pane kind since workspace format 6) repaints on the store's revision, copies the rows in view under a `try_lock` into a fixed buffer (a busy store redraws the last frame) and paints every cell into a stack string (no allocation), mirrors the columns around the strike (calls: OI, volume, IV, delta, last, bid, ask; puts the reverse from bid), leaves out whole columns from the outside in on a narrow pane (bid, ask and the strike always stay), draws the underlying's price as a row of its own between the strikes it falls between, and shades each side's in-the-money cells. It skips a frame whose bounds are empty (a first frame can be empty and far off: stepping a float across it never ends).
- Paper options (decision 0028, phase 3; `paper.rs`, `risk::check_option`, `core::settle_options`): the feed bridges forward each option quote to its own series (`MarketInput::OptionQuote`; a store's chains seed the core when it starts following). An option fills only on its own quote, as a one-level book of unlimited size: an order crossing it takes it at the quote's price; a resting limit fills at its limit when a later quote goes through it (touch: also at it); prints on the option or its underlying never fill. A missing side (none, zero, or a crossed quote) is empty, so nothing fills on it, and a market order (Flatten) works on until a quote has that side, never filling at a last price. A quote older than `risk::option_quote_stale_ms` (10 s on a series expiring that market day, 60 s on a later one; nothing saves or overrides it, and the core, paper and the chain all use it) fills nothing: an order that would cross it rests, a market order works on. Orders and fills only in the regular session. Before the broker, the core refuses option orders on a non-simulated broker (live is phase 4, an owner decision), on an expired series and on a prop-rule account (prop rules don't apply: such an account opens none, and the contract cap doesn't count options), and the guard (`RiskLimits::option_guard`, default when absent, never off) refuses market orders unless allowed, every stop and bracket, and a limit farther from the mid than its percentage or tick floor, on placing and moving it. An option order that would take a position through flat to the other side is refused (`risk::through_flat`, one definition for the core and the ticket, which asks it after its other checks as the core does; every order working on that side counts, exits, Flatten and orders with no known outcome included, since one closing first would leave this one to open the other side; rules' orders too, and an Entry that would cross a short is refused when armed as well as when it fires): long 1, a sell of 5 is an accident, so the message names Close and what fits. Opening from flat, a short included, is allowed on purpose (the owner's ruling). A broker's rejection keeps the broker's own words on the order (`OrderState::Rejected`), never a generic one, and leaves nothing working; a fired Stop or Target whose order is rejected says it on its row and tries again (a Stop on a new quote, a Target at its next chase), and a fired Entry whose order is rejected is reported ended again with the broker's words. Without a two-sided fresh quote there is no mid, so a limit is refused: typed blind, it could fill far from the market on the next quote. The one exception is a reduce-only limit at the side of a fresh quote that closes it (`risk::check_option_exit`), which needs no mid and no other side. A quote older than the newest is ignored, and an empty one keeps the last mark. A quote stamped ahead of the clock counts as stamped now, so it still goes stale and freezes nothing. Guardrails apply as to any order (an option's close is the regular session's). Flatten and cancels pass the guard; Flatten is never sent for an option on a non-simulated broker, and setting prop rules is refused while the account holds options or option orders. At the expiry's regular close (`option_close`) the core cancels the series' orders and settles positions at intrinsic against the underlying's last price before the close, through `Broker::settle` (no fee; simulated brokers only; one try per run); the service ticks before a batch reaches the marks, so a print the clock has already passed never counts. With no underlying price from the 24 hours before the close the position waits for the next price and settles on that.
- Option triggers (decision 0029; `crate::trigger`, `core/trigger.rs`): the core holds them and only a trade of the underlying in the option's regular hours fires one, so a stopped feed can't, a print more than `OptionGuard::trigger_stale_ms` old can't, and `TriggerView::stale` reports it. They issue ordinary commands as `Source::Rule`, past the same gates: an entry is one `PlaceOrder` limit at the ask (every new-order check at that moment; a refusal or an ask over the cap ends it), canceled if still working `OptionGuard::entry_fill_ms` (10 s, at least 1 s) after it fired, even if a person moved it, with the ended Entry reported again carrying why; a fired Stop is `Flatten` of the series (why it may be a market order, and why it can't open, grow or flip), a fired Target is `PlaceExit` plus `ModifyOrder` at most every `target_chase_ms`. Exits take the position's size when they act and end when it goes flat or flips. A print after a gap (stale, or the market opening) past a level fires an exit and ends an entry. A person's Cancel All ends the triggers it covers; a person's Flatten ends only entries, and a Stop or Target ends by itself once the position is flat, so a Flatten that can't close leaves it protecting. So a trigger never depends on a pane, the service layer keeps a feed running for each underlying with a live trigger (`FeedBridge::hold_underlyings`, driven by the app's trading pump; `TradingCore` itself stays free of I/O). It's the subscription a pane on that product has (a product target), so the core doesn't get that feed's data twice; a pane saved on an equity contract target runs a feed of its own, and then both reach the core (known gap). The workspace's status line speaks only when a live trigger can't fire or no pane on screen shows it (`chain_lines::trigger_status`). Every `rebuild` publishes the live ones, so a restored exit's notice reaches a subscriber that wasn't listening yet. Saved: Stops and Targets only (trading file from v4), held by `restore_triggers` until the first `rebuild` brings the positions back. A fired exit's commands pass the account's risk limits too (`Triggers::exempt`): they block entries, never exits. A fired exit ends its partner and any entry armed on the series. Triggers are refused on prop-rule accounts, venues and strategies. They and strategies never share a contract, so a trigger never trades under a strategy: a trigger won't arm where an instance is armed, paused or holds its own position, and an instance won't arm where a trigger is alive. A fired entry's resting limit is an order like a person's: an instance armed after it can have a fill it doesn't own.
- The chain's ticket (`chain_ticket.rs` builds and checks orders with no UI; `chain_view.rs` draws it): nothing moves a typed or defaulted limit once shown, so the send button always says what Enter sends; a sell with no long to sell reads `Sell to open 1 at 2.41`, as state. The ticket asks the same check as the core (`check_option`, or `check_option_exit` for a close) before sending, with the account's own guard (`AccountInfo::option_guard`), and shows the core's words; the core still decides. A row's Close and a ticket in Close mode are `PlaceExit` only (reduce-only, held to the position). Flatten and Cancel are the DOM's commands and keys, one per series of the underlying the account has anything on (every expiry, nothing else), never gated by the feed, quote or One-click; a read-only account alone refuses them. They read the cache with `try_lock` like the rest of the pane: busy, they say so and the press is repeated. One-click is off in every new pane, never saved, and off again on a symbol or account change; only a single click in the already focused pane sends. Enter only opens the ticket or sends what it shows, and a held Enter does neither (`workspace.rs`'s rule for orders). A click that closes the picker presses nothing. Accepted isn't filled: the pane watches its orders and shows a broker's rejection. The rows don't scroll while the selected strike is in view, and with One-click on the same strikes stay in the same places until One-click goes off, Home or another expiry: `Frame` holds the strikes and the price row's slot, the view pins the expiry, quotes are looked up each frame, and keys move only among those strikes, so a newly listed strike isn't shown or reached until then. A press lands where it was aimed. The Pos column, the header, the strip's slot and the footer keep their sizes for the same reason: an order landing moves no row.
- `.lfr` is format **v3**, or **v4** only when the contract is an equity or option (decision 0028; the contract kinds are all v4 adds): header = provider, protocol, catalog product symbol, contract key. A futures or spot recording is byte-for-byte what earlier builds wrote, so they still replay it; a v3 file claiming an equity or option contract is damaged. v2 files still read, with the contract unknown (shown as "contract unknown"; asking a v2 file for a specific contract is an error). v1 is rejected. Replay refuses unknown providers or protocols and product/contract mismatches. A recording holds exactly one contract: if the front month rolls mid-recording, recording stops. Imported data uses provider `lines` / protocol `lines-events-1`. Each frame is one atomic batch, depth included, so the store applies it under one lock and checks for a crossed book only at frame boundaries. A frame's time is the header's start plus its offset, worked out once by the reader (`Record::wall_ms`, checked): a record whose time doesn't fit an `i64` is skipped and counted (`Reader::unrepresentable`; a backtest warns with the market frames' count, replay and a levels file log theirs), never wrapped.
- Recording from the app ("Start recording" / "Stop recording": palette, Pane › Recording, the footer's Stop recording; `lines_feed::live_record`, app `recording.rs`): every running live feed writes the same `.lfr` `--record` writes, to `<data dir>/recordings/<source>-<symbol>-<UTC time>.lfr`; the source is the header's provider and the name's first word (decisions 0010, 0030: a recording of a licensed source is stamped with it, and is never attached anywhere). A start is a flag and a wake-up on the feed's `ChartStore::recording`: the feed thread reconnects once (`ConnectionEnd::Restart`, no backoff) and creates the file at that connect, so it holds a whole session and replays; a stop finishes the file on its writer thread while the connection goes on. Neither waits on the feed or the disk on the UI thread. A replay is never recorded again (`RecordCtl::recordable`). Live level sources record beside them (`levels-<UTC time>/`, the `--record-levels` layout, `levels::runtime::record_into`): each file starts with the source's newest whole batch (never a series-only push) at the time it came, so a replay has the map from its first moment. While recording, every window's footer status shows a red dot, "REC" and the elapsed time as h:mm:ss (text, not the dot alone) and "Stop recording"; the footer is always shown (its safety zone never hides), so starting or stopping moves no pane; it re-renders once a second for the time (render, not paint). A start that can't create its file is reported inline once (`RecordCtl::take_error`); level recording ends when no feed records any more; a stop during a start's reconnect calls it off; a feed that has stopped can't be recorded (`RecordCtl::close`). Screenshots (`--show-recording SECS`) show the state without recording anything.
- Sierra import is deterministic (same inputs → byte-identical file). Trades are checked against the book at emit time, never against look-ahead state. Sweep fills (Sierra unbundled trades) are not expected at the touch.
- Products vs contracts (decision 0013): the store is keyed by contract (`ChartData::contract`). `MarketEvent::ContractChanged(key)` retires the old contract's bars (kept, unadjusted, for continuous charts), never mixes them. Front month = `roll::resolve_front`: volume crossover when prior-session volumes are given, else the product's calendar rule, never backwards, never an expired contract. Live feeds following a product re-resolve on every (re)connect. Nothing rolls positions.
- Trading sessions (decision 0017, `lines_core::sessions`): a session belongs to its trade date; the ETH session of trade date D opens at `eth_open` on the calendar day before D (CME's Sunday 17:00 CT open is Monday's session) and closes on D; with same-day hours (`eth_open` before `eth_close`: US equities, 04:00–20:00 ET) it opens and closes on D, and halt days are refused (closed or early only). Hours are exchange-local in an IANA zone (jiff, bundled tzdb, so results never depend on the machine). Holidays/early closes are versioned data (`lines-feed/data/exchange-holidays.txt`, format 1, every date `verified` or `unverified` with sources); a `halt` holiday is not a trade date and its morning belongs to the next trade date's session (`Session::halt`), at most one per gap. Outside a table's coverage weekdays are assumed regular and sessions report `HolidayData::NotCovered`. Session queries never allocate (`lines-core/tests/sessions_no_alloc.rs`).
- Display time zone (`lines_core::display_tz`, app `display_zone.rs`; Settings › Appearance › Time zone, `--time-zone`): every time shown (chart axis and crosshair tag, the readout's tick span, time & sales, the journal and its trade snapshots, strategy marks and the strategies pane, profile and level notes) is written on one zone: the exchange's (US Eastern, labeled "ET") by default, the OS zone, or an IANA zone from the bundled tzdb. It is display only: storage, recordings, logs, file names and session logic stay UTC, and sessions, the 18:00 ET VWAP reset, trade dates (Day and Week bars' labels) and expiries stay on the exchange's clock whatever the zone. Functions that write a time take a `&DisplayTz` (tests pass their own; the process-wide `current()` is UTC until the app sets it), and writing never allocates. In the chart's axis corner the zone sits beside "manual" (a frozen price scale) and yields to it when both don't fit (`chart_view::corner_layout`). The zone is named where a time stands alone (the chart's axis corner and crosshair tag, the time & sales and journal Time headers (by a name that holds all year: "ET", "UTC" or the IANA city, since rows can span a DST change), a snapshot's header, a note with a clock time, a strategy's last decision), not in table cells. A change bumps `display_tz::epoch()` and the P&L display epoch, so cached time text (chart labels, the journal, the strategies pane) is rebuilt. An unknown saved zone shows ET and says so.
- Continuous series are display-only (`continuous::build`): unadjusted by default, difference back-adjust as an option (exact, on the tick grid), no ratio adjustment.
- Sierra contract from the file name: 2-digit years are 20YY; a 1-digit year is the earliest matching year not before the file's last record.
- History + live stitch (`lines_core::stitch`, roadmap 4): a history snapshot is bars plus a *closed* `as_of` (includes every trade with `time <= as_of`, none after; CQG's `up_to_utc_time` definition). Live trades with `time <= as_of` are dropped as already counted; later ones are added; trades applied before a late snapshot overwrote their bar are re-applied from a bounded journal (500k trades while history is outstanding, then the newest 3 bars). Open/close follow trade time, not arrival order. Providers must supply a closed `as_of`: CQG `up_to_utc_time` (the mock never stamps a trade at or before its last snapshot); Coinbase candles closed at the bucket end, the forming bar rebuilt from REST trades de-duplicated against websocket trades by `trade_id`. REST lags the websocket by seconds, so id holes (REST newest .. `last_match`, and matches dropped mid-stream) are fetched by id (`trades?after=hi+1`), retried on the keepalive tick, and reported as a history gap after 6 attempts; a hole wider than 6 full pages (6,000 ids) is a gap at once, and holes shrink by the ids received, never by walking id space; ids 0 and `u64::MAX` are malformed (rejected), so id arithmetic never wraps; history is `Complete` only once the REST-to-websocket hole is closed. Every (re)connect requests history from the newest stored bar; the window is `FeedConfig::history_ms` (default 3 days). `ChartData::history` (`Loading`/`Complete`/`Incomplete`/`Unsupported`) is shown as header text.
- All CQG `sint64` times are ms offsets from `LogonResult.base_time`.
- Depth (roadmap 5): `MarketEvent::BookClear`/`BookLevel` (new total per price, 0 removes); one frame is one atomic batch. `MarketEvent::DepthUnavailable { reason }` says a source won't send depth on this feed (a plan without it): the store's depth capability goes false for the run, its book empties, paper's book clears too, and the DOM shows the reason (on its bottom row, so no price moves); a source may still send the inside after it. CQG subscribes at `LEVEL_TRADES_BBA_DOM` and maps `TYPE_BID`/`TYPE_ASK` quotes; a snapshot clears the book. The CQG mock keeps a 10-level, one-tick-wide book that shifts with its price walk and is never crossed at a frame boundary. Coinbase uses the public `level2_batch` channel (`level2` needs authentication). Kraken spot subscribes `book` at depth 100; the session keeps its own book truncated to that depth (Kraken expects clients to drop levels pushed past it) and checks Kraken's CRC-32 of the top 10 levels after *every* message (each field is the value in grid units, so the checksum string is integer digits, never a float). A mismatch is counted, clears the book at once (`BookClear`; a book known to be wrong is never shown), and resubscribes; updates are ignored until the fresh snapshot. Kraken Futures books have no checksum: their sequence number must advance by one, and a skip is the same clear-and-resync (counted as a resync, not a `Gap`: `Gap` means dropped trades to the store and the local data store). `FeedStats::{book_checksums_ok, book_checksum_mismatches, book_resyncs}` count all of it. Kraken sessions validate tick and lot size against Kraken's public metadata (`AssetPairs`, Futures `instruments`) before subscribing: a mismatch is a fatal `BadMetadata`. Kraken Futures candle *volumes* are float sums printed to 16 digits (`0.3359999999999999`); only they are snapped, and only within 1e-6 of a grid step (`kraken::num::units_from_float_text`); everything else Kraken sends is parsed exactly from its text. Trades carry `aggressor: Option<Aggressor>`: CQG `sales_condition` (HIT/SELL_SIDE → sell, TAKE/BUY_SIDE → buy), Coinbase from the maker side (opposite), Sierra from the import.
- The store's `tape` (8192 prints) and `vap` (volume at price since data start for the contract) take only trades the stitch counts as new, so a re-delivered trade is never counted twice. Coinbase delivers the forming bar's REST trades as a closed snapshot (every REST trade before the newest REST timestamp `t_n`, `as_of = t_n - 1 ms`; trades at `t_n` follow as trades), so a reconnect inside the same minute replaces the bar instead of adding to it.
- DOM ladder (`lines_core::ladder`): rows are integer ticks × grouping (`row = floor(price / (grouping × tick))`); grouping only sums exact `Qty`, never rounds. `build_ladder` scans only the visible price range into a fixed-capacity buffer: per frame it visits at most `3 × rows × grouping` book/volume levels whatever the book depth (`tests/depth_stress.rs` asserts this at max replay speed, plus builds paced by the 8 ms UI tick, not the feed). Centring follows the inside market when it leaves the middle half of the rows, never while the pointer is over the ladder; C re-centres. Locked/crossed books are labelled and outlined, not only tinted.
- Panes (`lines-app/src/pane.rs`): chart, DOM and T&S are independent; each is given (`PaneFeed`, theme), owns its view state, polls only its own store's revision and is rendered as a cached view, so a tick on one market repaints only the panes showing it. Charts and T&S poll `ChartStore::chart_revision`, which depth below the touch doesn't move (a busy book repaints only the DOM). Panes never reference each other; a symbol switch emits `FeedChanged` and the workspace forwards it to the pane's link group. Feeds are shared through `FeedRegistry` keyed by (source, product, contract, follows front month); the last `Subscription` dropped stops the feed on a background thread. The layout is a value (`layout::Layout<PaneId>`; saved as `Layout<PaneDoc>`), rendered from `Layout::place` so drawing and hit-testing share one geometry. UI zoom (`lines-app/src/zoom.rs`; Ctrl+= / Ctrl+− / Ctrl+0, the palette, Settings › Appearance; optional `ui_zoom` in settings, absent at 100 %): one process-wide factor, read without allocation or lock in paint, the same in every window (popped-out ones included). Element trees scale through each window's rem size (set at the top of every window's render) and `zoom::ui` for an explicit size; painted rows and text through `zoom::f` of their 100 % constant (`row_h()`, `label_px()`, `header_row_h()`, …). Layout *decisions* stay in 100 % units: the DOM trading panel gets the pane's size divided by the zoom (`trade_bar::panel_rows`, `is_narrow`), and minimum heights and widths are multiplied at their call sites; tests never set the zoom (they run in parallel at 100 %). A change bumps `zoom::generation`, and `text::Glyphs` and the chart's shaped labels drop what was shaped at the old size. Hit targets take `zoom::hit` (never below their 100 % size: 80 % shrinks text, never a 20 px target). Saved column widths are in 100 % units. Above 100 % a split keeps a DOM at least `trade_bar::MIN_DOM_WIDTH` × zoom wide (`Layout::place_where`'s `min_w`; at 100 % and below, layouts are unchanged). The chart's time and price zoom are separate (wheel and drag) and untouched.
- Workspaces (decision 0016): workspace → windows → tabs → layout → panes. Link groups (A–D, colour + letter, or unlinked) span all windows; joining a group adopts its market. Only the active tab of a visible window is rendered; every other pane is marked hidden and its poll doesn't notify, so hidden tabs and minimised windows do no paint work (`--bench` exits 2 if its hidden tab painted). Focus is always drawn (violet outline; dim when the window is inactive). Keys go through one capture-phase handler: `commands::command_for` (pure, tested) maps them to `Command`s, which the context menu also issues; pane keys (letters, C/G, Home, Ctrl+K, Ctrl+D, Alt+M) are never bound. Crosshair sync (opt-in per workspace, Ctrl+Shift+X) is keyed by link group (`tabstate::crosshair_peers`: same group, unlinked with unlinked): a chart emits `CrosshairTime` (time, price, product) after paint; its peers mark that bar (`geometry::time_slot`, missing in a gap). Optional price sync (`crosshair_sync_price`, palette/tab menu) also marks the price, only between charts of the same product (`tabstate::syncs_price`).
- Command palette order (`palette_rank`, owner-approved; the idea from Zed's palette, no code): commands run from the palette before come first, more uses first, then the rest by fuzzy score (`picker::Searcher::score_texts`, the symbol search's scorer); a command that doesn't match never shows, however often it ran. Every tie is broken by `commands::palette()` order, and the history is a snapshot taken when the palette opens (a run counts after it closes), so the same query always gives the same list and nothing moves under the pointer. Commands that trade or can't be taken back (`palette_rank::never_boosted`: every `Command::Trade`, the strategies' Start, Stop and Stop all, the chain's One-click, the update's restart and roll-back, Close pane) are never remembered (no count, no query), so a history never puts one at the top of the empty palette and ↑ never recalls the query that ran one. Saved in `settings.json` `palette` (optional, no migration): a use count per command keyed by its name (`{:?}`; a setting's by its settings key and value text, never its table position: a renamed or reordered command starts again at 0, never takes another's count) and the last 10 queries a command was run from, newest first, each once. ↑ on the first row of an empty palette recalls the newest query, ↑ again older ones while the recalled query is unchanged and its first row chosen (past the oldest, ↑ stays: it never wraps to the bottom row); anywhere else (the selection moved, a typed or edited query) ↑ and ↓ move the selection. Query characters are literal (no fuzzy-pattern operators).
- Hide / detach (roadmap 5b): a hidden or detached pane keeps its leaf, so its tab's layout keeps every ratio and showing or re-attaching restores the exact size and position (`Layout::place_where` gives a wholly hidden side's space to its sibling). Hidden panes keep their feed, link and settings and paint nothing (`--bench-hide-doms`). A detached pane stays owned by its tab (same link group, subscriptions) and is rendered by a `PaneWindow`; closing that window re-attaches it, never loses it. "Chart only" hides a chart's linked DOM/T&S in its tab as `chart_only` and restores exactly those; panes the trader hid stay hidden; detached ones are untouched. A tab always keeps one shown pane. A window shows its tab bar only with two tabs or more (`tabstate::shows_tab_bar`); the workspace status (the layout's name, crosshair sync, reports, hidden/popped-out panes) is always in the footer, and Ctrl+T, the pane menu and the palette make a second tab. Closed tabs go on an in-memory stack (10) as their saved form (feeds stop) and Reopen brings them back at their index; closing the last tab of the last window replaces it with a fresh tab. The tab strip (`tabstrip.rs` pure, `workspace::tab_bar`): tabs keep their fixed width and scroll sideways with the wheel when they don't fit, the active tab is kept in view (not on a click on it: the tab under the pointer never moves at the press), and a ▾ lists every tab; hover, clicks and drops use the strip's own geometry at the drawn (clamped) scroll, so the pointer hits what is drawn. Each tab's close slot is always there (× on the active and hovered tab, a pin on a pinned one), so a title never re-truncates under the pointer; after a click on ×, the × that slides into that slot takes no press until the pointer leaves the tab bar or the window (`tabstate::CloseGuard`; another tab's ×, aimed at, still closes it); meanwhile the strip holds its scroll, even past its new end (`Strip::held`), so no tab moves under the pointer. A drop inside this window's tab bar is held to the strip (`Strip::drop_x`), never into a tab scrolled out of view; beside or above the window the pointer is read as it is, so a drop on another window's tab bar still moves the tab there. An unavailable pane gets no feed badge (the pane says why). A background tab says ON (a DOM in it trades) and STALE, FAILED or STOPPED for a feed of its panes that isn't live (`tabstate::tab_feed_badge`; connecting is passing, and the footer names it), with its symbol in the tooltip, as words, never by color alone, read without waiting on a feed. A tab has an optional name (Rename tab…; empty: its symbols again) and can be pinned (`TabDoc::name`, `TabDoc::pinned`, optional: no version bump): Close other tabs and a middle click skip a pinned tab (`tabstate::close_others`, `middle_click_closes`), and Close other tabs never closes more than Reopen closed tab keeps (10: past that it says so and closes nothing), needs no confirmation because every tab it closes reopens, and is never boosted to the top of the palette (`palette_rank::never_boosted`); a layout preset keeps the tab's name and pin; its × slot shows the pin (Ctrl+W and Close tab still close it). While a tab is dragged over the strip a 2 px marker shows where it lands. Footer notices (`notice.rs`): the same message replaces itself with a count (`×3`); refusals expire after 6 s (one timer each, nothing ticking); problems that need action (files unreadable/unsaved) stay until clicked. Hover controls (pane header, tab ×) are built only for the pane/tab under the pointer, found from geometry on mouse move: the window root re-renders every frame, so per-pane chrome costs every frame (this was measured: ~25% on the 6+2 benchmark).

- Data folder (`data_dir.rs`; Settings › Data › Data folder): holds `store-v1/`, `recordings/`, `logs/`, `paper/`, `journal/` (with `journal/shots/`) and `backups/`; settings, workspaces and drawings stay in the config folder. Resolved once at startup, before the store, feeds, trading, the journal or the logs open: `LINES_DATA_DIR`, else the folder in the pointer file `<OS default>/location.json` (`lines_feed::disk::os_default_root`; versioned v1: `data_dir`, optional `pending_move { from }`), else the OS default. The pointer is read leniently (missing, unreadable, newer, or naming a folder that doesn't exist → the OS default and a notice; never created, never set aside). `paper/`, `journal/`, `backups/` each come from the data folder if there, else the config folder if there (installs from before the data folder; nothing moves on its own), else the data folder. Screenshots, the bench and backtests never read the pointer (`data_dir::run` falls back to `LINES_DATA_DIR`, else the OS default); headless runs read it but never finish a move (they use the folder being moved from). Folders are compared as the same file on Unix and by canonical path, case ignored, on Windows and macOS. A picked folder must not be the one in use, inside it or contain it, nor the settings folder, inside it or contain it (except the OS default: on macOS it is the settings folder), and must take a probe file. "Start empty" only writes the pointer; when the picked folder already holds Lines data it is offered as "Use its data" instead (U), the pending line says the data already there is used, and still only the pointer is written (nothing is copied into it or written over). "Move existing data" is refused, said inside the choice with Move and Start empty disabled, while any account has a position or a working order (`data_dir::move_refusal`, read from the trading cache when the choice shows and again at the press; never at startup), and refused into a folder already holding Lines data (any item with something in it; `data_dir::holds_data`, checked at the pick and again at the start when no record of this move exists). It writes the pointer with `pending_move` and copies `store-v1`, `logs`, `recordings` on its own thread (progress on the row, in GiB) while this run keeps the old folder, checking each file byte for byte right after copying it and recording, in `<OS default>/location.verified.json` (versioned v1: the move's `from`/`to`; `files`: each checked file's relative path, the source's size and modification time and the copy's own as its drive stored them (exFAT and FAT keep times in 10 ms or 2 s steps), each side compared with its own; `written`: every file the move created or replaced), before its first file, every few seconds and at its end; a file written to during its copy isn't recorded as checked, and one gone since the listing (a rotated log, a date the budget removed) is skipped. Pointer and record writes from Move, Start empty and Cancel are ordered (`data_dir::ordered`: one at a time, an older choice's write skipped). The next interactive start finishes it before anything opens. Deliberate rule (it departs from "check every file at the start", which stalled big stores): a recorded file still that size and time in both folders is trusted without re-reading; every other big-item file (missing, grown, changed in either folder, never checked) is copied if needed and checked byte for byte; `paper`/`journal`/`backups` (from wherever they live) are always copied and checked; a record for another move, or a damaged one, is no record. A file already in the target that the move didn't write is never replaced: it must be the same, or the move fails. Choosing a move writes the move's id into `<from>/.lines-move-source` (before the pointer, in the same ordered write; `pending_move.id`), and the start moves only from a folder holding that marker: one recreated empty at the path by a run while it was missing, another disk given its letter, or a bare mount point fails the move ("isn't the folder being moved from (its marker is missing)"), stays pending, and nothing is moved or deleted. The marker goes after a successful switch, and on Cancel (best effort). A source folder that isn't there fails the move too (it stays pending): the run keeps pointing at it, the row says plainly that this run uses an empty folder there and where the checked copy is, and the move finishes only when the folder with its marker is back; switching to the partial copy instead could run without the paper accounts and journal, and leave two diverging copies. An item whose source and target are one folder, or one inside the other, fails the move (nothing is removed). Then it switches the pointer and drops the record; only then remove the old copies (only the files it moved and writer locks; a folder only once empty; never the folder itself or anything else in it). Writer locks and `*.tmp` are never copied; a store whose writer lock another process holds isn't moved. The row's disk facts (whether the next folder is the one in use or holds data, whether the default is in use) are worked out off the UI thread when the pointer's folder changes and cached (a worker's result is kept only if its folder is still the one asked about) (`data_dir::facts` makes no disk call; startup and screenshots fill the cache before any window). - Chart layers (`chart_view::ChartLayer`): `prepare` under the store lock after the viewport update, `paint` inside the plot after the candles; neither may allocate. - Indicators (decision 0017): every feed's batches reach the indicator thread through `indicators::EngineTap` (beside the store writer); the engine runs `lines_indicators::Graph` in feed order (deterministic). A feed's base series (`Spec::Market`) lives as long as its `ChartStore` (a finished replay can still get indicators). Identical (feed, spec) requests share one node. Consumers get an `IndicatorHandle` whose `OutputCell` the engine updates in place (only changed points) with an atomic revision; panes poll it via `Pane::changed` and `try_lock` in paint. VWAP: accumulators are exact `i128` (`3pv`, `9p²v`, `v`, so bar typical prices `(H+L+C)/3` stay integer); a bucket is exact only if its trades all arrived as trades (after the newest snapshot's `as_of`, or from a replay's first recorded frame (`Input::TradesFrom`), no gap/disconnect), else bar-derived and the point is flagged approximate until the next reset; at startup the engine also gets the store's cached bars (`Input::Cached`, bar-derived) and its stored trades with their coverage (`Input::Stored`: trade sums only, never bars; the covered ranges are complete); the value is `f64`, never an order price. Resets come from the sessions calendar (ETH default, RTH, week, month of trade dates) or an anchor bar. Drawn by `studies.rs` beside the `ChartLayer`s (same prepare/paint points; the trait is unchanged) as a smooth anti-aliased path through bar centres plus band polygons; GPUI tessellates those paths per frame (its allocation), the column geometry is allocation-free. - Footprint (roadmap 10e, `lines_core::footprint`, app `footprint.rs`): a chart bar's cells are the merge of the store's 1-minute footprints over its bucket `[bar.time, BarsView::end(i))`, never a separate per-timeframe store, so every timeframe sums the same trades (tested against footprints counted directly at 5m/15m/1h). Trade-built bars (tick, volume, range, seconds) are the exception: their bucket boundaries fall inside minutes, so each such bar keeps its own per-price volume from the same counted trades (`footprint::bar_levels`, tested against a footprint counted per bar). Counted trades only. POC uses `ProfileRows::build` (the profile's tie rule); imbalances compare diagonally (`buy[r]` vs `sell[r−1]`, `sell[r]` vs `buy[r+1]`; an empty opposite counts as 0, the own side must reach the minimum, in whole quantity units) and are marked by shape and position (an underline under the imbalanced side's number, never at the column's left edge where the vertical open/close strip is), not color alone. Volume without an aggressor side is never dropped silently: a cell of only such volume prints `?N` (never `0 | 0`) and the legend counts it. `FootprintFrame::build` checks the trades' own row extent against its buffer before `ProfileRows::build`, so it never grows it. **A drawn cell always carries its numbers, whole**: `prepare` keeps a bar as cells only if its whole column is on the plot, every number fits its room (`numbers_fit`, from the font's measured tabular digit widths; quantities print through `QtyFormat`, so crypto decimals count), rows are at least `MIN_TEXT_ROW_PX` tall, and no cell lies under the readout/legend block (`set_legend_block`, from the last render); otherwise the bar is its candle and `Fallbacks` says why in the legend (the chart re-renders when they change; a bar cut at the plot's edge beside drawn cells needs no words, and the legend carries no key). Never a shaded cell without its number (it would read as nothing traded). Auto-fit raises the range's top clear of that block (`clear_top`). Horizontal price/crosshair lines break across drawn cells (`hline_segments`). Tested with Kraken 8-decimal quantities and ES 1h/1D volumes (`check_drawable`).

- Volume (owner-approved 2026-11-09; `lines_core::volume`, drawn by `studies.rs`): a bar's total is the chart bar's own `volume`, never a separate count, so it agrees with its candle on every timeframe, tick bars included; it needs no engine node (`IndicatorConfig::spec` is `None`). - External lower-pane series (decision 0022's extension: an external source can provide lower-pane line series; `lines_core::levels::Series`, `LevelSource::series_studies`, app `series_pane.rs`): a level source's map may carry line series (a key, ascending points, disjoint segments; at most 16, validated with the map: a malformed series refuses the map like a bad level), and the source declares the studies it offers (`SeriesStudyDef`: id, name, lines with a theme color role and a line type each, an optional dashed zero line, a stale threshold). Contract and the PAPER/LIVE badge never give way; the status word is never dropped (when the badge leaves no room for it the link chip goes first, and only then does it truncate with "…"); the account chip drops to its PAPER or LIVE badge; the timeframe chip drops before it, except while its list is open; the description truncates to 48 px, then drops whole; the quotes drop item by item (bid and ask together). A test keeps every product, timeframe (tick presets in both modes and the widest chip, 100000t continuous with 99999 left, included) and default account before the gutter at `CHART_HEADER_MIN_W`. Tick bars (roadmap 9b, `lines_core::ticks`, `lines-feed/src/ticks.rs`; one builder for every kind, `BarKind`: `Ticks(n)` trades, `Volume(n)` at least n whole contracts (`BarScale::qty_unit`; the reaching trade never split), `Range(r)` never wider than r price ticks by integer arithmetic (done at exactly r; a trade that would widen it opens the next bar at its price; no bars invented across a gap), `Seconds(n)` UTC-aligned slots starting at the slot and spanning it (a late trade joins the newest bar); "tick bars" below means all four): built only from the counted trades, the same set as the tape, `vap` and footprints in the same order: the stored trades the warm start replays (`ChartData::apply_stored`), then the live trades the stitch counts (`ChartData::apply`), by one `TickBars` per (store, kind, reset) (`TickKey`) shared through `TickLease`s (dropped with the last lease). They keep bars, not trades, capped at the series' `max_bars` (dropped in chunks, counted as trimmed); a steady-state trade allocates nothing (`no_alloc.rs`). Session resets (the default) close the forming bar at each ETH open and a holiday halt's reopen (a trade between sessions belongs to the next, as the store partitions; no calendar: the UTC day); sessions never go backwards (a late trade joins the forming bar). The newest bar short of its size is *forming* until its session part closes on the feed's clock, then *partial* (a seconds bar: forming until its slot ends, then simply done, never partial for that) (`is_forming_at`/`is_partial_at` with `ChartStore::feed_clock`, never the wall clock, so replays and screenshots stay deterministic). A bar's span for time mapping runs to the next bar's start within a session, else to just after its last trade (a seconds bar: exactly its slot, so a time in a slot without trades maps to no bar). A chart open at startup gets the stored sessions' bars as they replay. A chart asking later (`acquire_ticks`) gets bars from the tape when it holds every counted trade (`counted_n`); while the warm start runs it is deferred ("building") and rebuilt when it ends (`end_orderflow_loading`); else a helper thread reads the trades before the tape again: first the warm start's stored trades by its record (`StoredReplay`: the same segments and covered ranges at or before `stored_through`; the total must match, else the store changed), then the live ones from `ChartData::tick_source` (a replay's file through the same stitch and `stored_through` floor, cut after the right count; or the store's segments, which hold exactly the trades live counted: those after `stored_through`, cut where the tape's first 64 prints appear in order, only if exactly the right number came before), then the tape, then the trades held meanwhile (at most 1M), so no trade is counted twice. Splits are by position, never by time. Every way a rebuild can fail (a count that doesn't match, such as stored trades this process never counted; an unreadable source; no thread; a panic: the `Rebuilding` guard) falls back to the tape with the late label, never a stuck "building", and lowers `REBUILDS` (screenshots wait on it). Held trades are applied off the store's lock in chunks (at most 4096 under it). The late label (date and the display zone, like the crosshair tag) shows when the first bar starts after trading the 1-minute bars show in its session (continuous: any earlier 1-minute bar), or, when the 1-minute bars don't reach back before that session, more than 60 s after its open; `TickSet::status` is cached per builder revision and 1-minute extent (charts ask every paint). Across a switch (`keep_view`) the pointer's time waits for bars while a rebuild shows none, and the landed series sets a new trim baseline. A restored chart takes its saved tick mode (`ChartState.tick_continuous`: saved wins; absent keeps `--tick-continuous`). Footprint and the visible-range profile read the bars' own per-price volume (`footprint::bar_levels`); VWAP and cumulative delta take the engine's 1-minute point holding each bar's last trade.

- No heap allocation in our paint path (`lines-core/tests/no_alloc.rs`, counting allocator: chart, and DOM/T&S rows and formatting). Chart label text is shaped once per value and cached; a cache miss allocates. DOM and T&S numbers are painted from per-character glyphs shaped once (`text::Glyphs`), so new values don't allocate. - Local store (`lines-feed/src/disk`, decision 0019): per contract `store-v1/<source>/<contract>/<trade date>.lseg`, append-only **segment v1** (v2 only for an equity or option contract, as `.lfr` v4; header like `.lfr` v3/v4 + source, encoding, trade date; blocks `u32 len | u32 crc32 | i64 time_us | lines-events-1`, one block per feed frame). Opening a segment for writing truncates at the first invalid block (torn tail; never repaired); readers stop at an incomplete block without consuming it, so they run alongside the writer. One writer per contract, exclusive via `writer.lock`: the feed's `ContractWriter` (`EventSink` in `connection.rs`; file I/O on its own thread), or a downloaded day's `disk::day::DayWriter` ("Download days…", decision 0030 §8), which holds the lock for its whole day, so a contract a live feed is storing is refused (`Busy`, shown by the estimate up front: the live writer keeps coverage and the bar index in memory and would overwrite what a download added). A live feed that finds its contract locked (a day downloading) tries again every 10 s of feed time (`StoreSink`); a writer opened then stores that connection's trade segments only (`ContractWriter::open_segments_only`: no torn-tail check or bar rebuild on the feed thread, never the bar index or coverage, which the chart's bars would overwrite), and the next connection opens a full writer (cached bars older than the chart's loaded, coverage claimed from its complete history). The estimate's lock probe holds the lock for an instant: a day writer tries again once, a feed at its next try. A downloaded day is written whole or not at all: blocks go to `<date>.lseg.part` (no reader or retention sees that extension), and only a commit stores it, in an order whose crash windows are harmless: pin the date (the contract's `downloaded` list), claim its session's trades (read only with the segments that exist), rename the part into place, then merge its 1-minute bars into the bar index and only then claim its bars (an index that can't be saved is removed and rebuilt; no index, no bars claim); a failure before the rename takes the pin and claim back; a stop, a refusal, a store error or a day with no market data deletes the part file and claims nothing. Cancel remaining and closing the window (dropping a `Job`) start no further day: the one in flight, maybe already billed, finishes and is stored. Only Stop now (`Cancel::cancel`; a second press at least 400 ms after Cancel remaining, never a held key) discards it, and only before all its paid data has arrived (`DaySink::fetched`: from then on `canceled()` reads false and the day is converted and stored whatever is pressed; an error then is a failure); a day is stored only if the source returned Ok and the sink never stopped it. A source calls `DaySink::requesting` right before its first request that may be billed (after free checks); the "charged" figure counts every paid day from there (`Update::Requested`; a source that never says so is counted once any bytes or events arrived), finished or not, so it never reads lower than the bill. Pinned days are never evicted (a paid day is fetched once) but count toward the budget; the dialog says when new days may pass it. Only finished trade dates (session closed) with no segment at all are downloaded: a stored day (live or downloaded, complete or partial) is never estimated, requested or appended to again (`download::plan`). Events outside the trade date's session are dropped and counted. A download re-checks each day's price first and never fetches above the price the person accepted. Sources implement `lines_feed::download::PastDays` (versioned: `API_VERSION`, refused at `register` when it differs); its `estimate` and `download` block and run only on a `Job`'s thread (which also reads the store's plan), never the UI's; `contents` is called on the UI thread and must answer at once. A job's thread that dies still ends with `Finished`. A provider's refusal (`DayError::Refused`) is shown in its own words; local and conversion failures are `Failed`. The dialog's Download needs a fresh press at least 600 ms after the estimate shows (a held Enter never acts), so the press that asked for the cost can't start a paid download. Flush policy: write when 256 KiB are buffered or every 200 ms, `sync_data` at most every 1 s and on close (crash loses ≤ 200 ms, power loss ≤ ~1.2 s). Stored live: trades (with aggressor) and best bid/ask; depth only for `--store-depth` products (a downloaded day stores what its contents chose: "Full depth" days hold book levels whatever `--store-depth` says); never option quotes (a chain is a live snapshot: after a restart it comes from the provider again). Not stored: provider history (it lives in the bar index), so a stored session replays its *trades*.
- Bar index (`bars`, CRC'd, replaced atomically) is a cache of the chart's bars, saved every 30 s and at each disconnect; rebuilt from segments if missing or damaged. Startup: the feed thread loads it into the chart store before connecting (panes show it at once), then requests history from the earliest hole in the bar coverage inside the window (else the newest bar); `lines_core::stitch` merges without double counting.
- Stored order flow (`disk::orderflow`, `lib.rs::warm_start`): after the bar cache loads and before connecting, the feed thread replays the stored trades of the newest `FeedConfig::orderflow_sessions` stored trade dates (default 2, 0 = off) in one pass over the segments into the tape, VAP, profiles and tick-bar builders (`ChartData::apply_stored`: never the bars, which already hold them; `counted_n` counts them with the live ones; `stored` keeps what was replayed, so a tick-bar rebuild reads exactly those trades again) and the indicator engine. Only trades inside the trade coverage count, and the segments hold exactly the trades live counted: `ChartData::apply` records per frame which trades it counted (`frame_counted`) and the writer, right after on the feed thread, stores only those (a reconnect's re-sent trades a snapshot covers are not stored; a connection's late trades, a REST fill after newer streamed ones, are), so a stored session replays to what live built; bars rebuilt from segments are claimed complete only where trades are; a corrupt or torn segment withdraws its coverage after the last good block; a segment evicted meanwhile is skipped. `stored_through` (store and engine) is the newest replayed trade's exchange time (never the coverage end, which follows the local clock) and drops a live trade at or before it, so a history snapshot older than the store never counts a trade twice. Panes show bars first and the header says `loading order flow…` (`ChartData::orderflow_loading`); trades go in 20k slices so the store lock is held for milliseconds. Replays and screenshots use no store unless `--store-dir` names one.
- Coverage (`coverage`, text): `bars` and `trades` ranges known complete. Claimed by a committed downloaded day (its whole session) or, live, only after `HistoryComplete`: bars from the requested history start, trades from the newest snapshot `as_of` + 1 ms, both to the newest of feed time and trade time. A `Gap` closes the claim; a `HistoryGap` is excluded; a disconnect ends it. Trade claims count only where the segment file still exists (eviction) and stop at the last valid block after a torn-tail cut. Coinbase reports history beyond its page budget as a `HistoryGap`.
- Partition = session trade date via `disk::partition::trade_date` (from `catalog::sessions`; data outside every session goes to the next session's date; products without a calendar use the UTC date).
- Retention: oldest trade dates first across all contracts until segments fit the budget (default 4 GiB); segments being written and downloaded (pinned) days are never deleted.
- Stale (docs/design.md §7): `ChartStore::stale_for()` (lock-free) is `Some` while the feed is `Live`, its market open, and nothing arrived for longer than `stale_after` (5 s; CQG 10 s; `FeedConfig::stale_after` overrides). Panes poll it with their revision, show a `STALE · no data N s` badge, and the DOM and T&S dim. Market-open comes from the product's sessions calendar, evaluated at the feed's clock (last market time + silence), so a closed market is never stale and replays stay deterministic.
- Latency HUD (roadmap 6b; `lines_core::latency`, app `latency_hud.rs`): off by default (`Workspace::show_latency_hud`, optional saved field), mirrored in the process-wide `latency::enabled()`; while it is off nothing records (one relaxed load per feed frame and per paint), a chart drops its receive → paint window and a feed's `ChartData::latency` is freed on its next frame; the trading core's two order windows (~62 KB) exist from its start and keep their samples across a toggle (`tests/latency_hud.rs` in lines-feed and lines-trading). Windows are 60 one-second histograms of quarter-octave buckets, keyed by the caller's clock in ms: feed exchange → receive on the frame's receive time (a replay's recorded time, so replays and screenshots are deterministic), order click → sent and the ack round trip (first ack of a `Submitted` order, `at − created`) on the trading core's clock, receive → paint on the monotonic clock from the last update that moved `chart_revision` (depth below the touch doesn't reset it), recorded only for frames that draw the data; while the pane is hidden its poll marks updates seen (`Pane::on_hidden_tick`), so a hidden tab's wait is not paint latency. The order windows read at the newer of the core's clock and the chart's feed clock, so they age out while the core idles. Back-fill is not latency: HTTP frames and frames a session flags with `ProviderSession::backfill_frame` (Kraken's trade snapshots) are left out, and so is any trade more than 60 s late; a session that streams past trades must flag them. Every lock is copied from and dropped before text is built; the trading core and the UI only `try_lock` the order windows (decision 0012). A quantile is its bucket's upper edge (never below the true value, at most 25% above). Feed and ack are whole milliseconds (`<1` below that); a feed whose samples are all exactly 0 with a clock-offset bound of exactly 0 has no separate exchange time and reads `n/a` (a clock behind the exchange also records 0s, but a negative bound). The clock line is `ClockOffset`'s bound `min(receive − exchange)` for wall-clock (live) feeds only, with a ⚠ badge (text, not color alone) beyond ±250 ms. Recording and reading never allocate (`no_alloc.rs`); the header text is rebuilt at render at most once a second, and the screenshot path switches recording on before it replays. A frozen frame leaves out the wall-clock measures (paint, order).
- Reset paper account (`core/reset.rs`, `Command::ResetPaper`; app `paper_reset.rs`): never by deleting the file under a live core. The core flattens and cancels everything on the account first (the same Flatten a person sends) and resets only once the account is flat with no order open or unresolved (`Event::ResetWaiting`, then `Event::AccountReset`; a waiting reset never goes off later on its own: it is dropped with `Event::ResetDropped` when its flatten ends with the position still open or a new order is placed on the account): the broker's balance and positions, then the core's balance, positions and prop-rule progress (high-water mark, today's P&L, breaches). Guardrails, their counts and any lockout are kept, and the day's loss from before the reset still counts against the daily-loss guardrail and the risk daily loss (`GuardrailTracker::carried`, until the next trade date; every place that computes the day's P&L for a limit adds it). A broker account (any broker that isn't simulated) is refused by name, in the app before anything arms and in the core; a strategy can't ask. The service keeps the trading file as `<source>-YYYYMMDD-HHMMSS.json.bak` (UTC) when the reset is asked for, before anything is saved over it. Every way in (palette, the account menu, Settings › Accounts) takes a second press, at least `paper_reset::READ_AT_LEAST` after the first (a double-click doesn't send) and within `paper_reset::CONFIRM`, after a first that names the account, source and balance; the journal is kept unless the trader chose to clear it, which the journal does on `Event::AccountReset { clear_journal }`, in order with the events, so trades after the reset stay (see Trade journal).
- Flatten (`core/flatten.rs`): it cancels everything and closes, but never gives up an exit the broker caps for a close that doesn't happen. On a broker honoring reduce-only (`BrokerCaps::oco`) the market order goes first and only non-protective orders are cancelled at once; the position's exits (reduce-only orders and bracket legs, which the broker caps) stay until it is flat (`cancel_stranded_exits`, and the broker's own reduce-only). A plain stop is cancelled at once: it could reverse the position in the moment after the fill (decisions 0025, 0027). On one that doesn't, everything is cancelled first and the market order goes when those are answered, at most `FLATTEN_ANSWER_MS` later, for what the position is then, and a person's Cancel All takes back a person's one still waiting (a rule's stays: it asks once). It doesn't go beside an earlier Flatten's order with a word in that time, and a broker disconnecting ends it as a rejected order saying so: nothing goes later on a position not yet rebuilt. In the paper broker a Flatten's market order neither draws on nor is limited by the exits' reduce-only budget, now or while it works on (a thin book, an option with no usable quote).
- Reverse (`core/reverse.rs`, `Command::Reverse`, a person's only): one step, never one order of twice the size a stale exit could overfill. It cancels every order on the contract (on a broker honoring reduce-only the position's protective exits the core knows are reduce-only stay until it is flat; a leg never reported reduce-only is cancelled at once, unlike a Flatten, which keeps by role), then sends two market orders: the close, a Flatten's own (`Role::Flatten`, reduce-only), and the opening, an entry for the position's size (with its bracket in per-entry mode; position mode's pair follows the flip). Neither can leave a doubled position: the close stops at flat and the opening is only ever the old size. Where reduce-only isn't honored nothing new goes until the cancels are answered (at most `FLATTEN_ANSWER_MS`); past that wait the close goes and the opening is refused with a reason (an unanswered order could fill beside it). The opening is an entry: guardrails and prop rules check the whole reversal (the orders it cancels left out), risk limits the opening alone from flat; refused, nothing is sent and the reason says so. After a wait it is checked again (refused then, the close still goes). One at a time: refused while a Reverse is waiting or has an order working, and while a Flatten is closing the position; a Flatten (anyone's) or a person's Cancel All takes back a waiting one, which says so as a refused order, and a Flatten during one cancels its opening if that cancel lands before it fills. A position closed while it waited opens nothing; an order placed during the wait is cancelled and waited on too, under the same cap, and one whose cancel the broker refused, or that a rebuild left with no known outcome meanwhile, refuses the opening while the close goes; a reduce-only exit kept to protect the position doesn't (it can't add to the new side). An order with no known outcome at the press refuses the whole Reverse, nothing sent (a Reverse can't cancel it). In the paper broker the opening stays an entry beside its close: a market order counts as closing only for what closing market orders already working leave of the position. Disconnected, refused whole. Entry triggers armed on the contract end ("you reversed").
- Trading core (`lines-trading`, roadmap 6; decisions 0004/0005/0006/0012/0018): every order action is a `Command` in an `Envelope` (id, `Source`, the input event's `Instant`) through `TradingHandle::submit`; each gets exactly one `Event::Command` result (`Ok(order ids)` or a `Reject` with a `RejectCode` and message). Everything else comes out as `Event`s on `TradingHandle::subscribe` (Order, Fill, Position, Account, Breach, Guardrail, Strategy, Rebuilt), in order. `TradingCore` is pure (time passed in, no I/O) and runs on the `TradingService` thread; it never waits on a lock the UI holds: the only lock it shares is the latency HUD's `OrderLatency`, which both sides only `try_lock` (a sample that meets the UI reading is dropped). Order states move only along `OrderState::can_become` (Submitted → Acked → Working → PartiallyFilled → Filled / Cancelled / Rejected with a reason); nothing is Working before its ack. Money is exact: `Money(i128)` in 1e-10 currency units, `ContractSpec::money_per_unit` must divide exactly; positions are FIFO lots, so realised P&L is exact. Risk (`RiskLimits`: max order qty, max position counting open orders, price band in ticks from the last trade else the mid, daily loss) and prop rules run before the broker; `CancelOrder`, `CancelAll` and `Flatten` never pass through them. Open orders (`open_qty`) include live bracket exits and count an OCO pair once; only exits held for a working entry with nothing live (never activated, or their sibling closed what was open) are left out, and the strategy gate adds those held exits on top. So a manual close of a bracketed position counts its live exits as already closing: in a guardrail cooldown or reducing only it is checked as an entry (Flatten stays the way out). A `ModifyOrder` that adds quantity is a new order for the increase (guardrails, trading state, contract cap, risk, submit rate) and the whole order meets the order-size limit; a moved price meets the band, a stop's trigger too. Prop rules are data (`RulePreset`, labelled "user-editable approximation", no firm names): trailing drawdown intraday (equity high-water) or end-of-day (closing balances), optional lock at start + X, daily loss per trade date, micro-equivalent contract cap (`Product::micro_units`: a mini by its micro relation, a micro 1, every other future 10 whether or not it has a micro partner, spot and equities their quantity), profit target, consistency; a breach blocks new orders and, with `liquidate_on_breach`, flattens (`Source::Rule`). Day boundaries are session trade dates (`disk::partition::trade_date`) of the account's `day_product`. Reconnect/restart: `rebuild` replaces balances, positions and open orders from `Broker::snapshot` (a contract it no longer lists is reported flat); orders the broker doesn't know become Cancelled. Accounts a broker discovers after connecting join with `add_accounts` (`TradingHandle::add_accounts`), which rebuilds. `BrokerEvent::ExternalFill` (an execution on an order Lines didn't place) becomes an `Event::Fill` with order `EXTERNAL_ORDER` (0) and no realised P&L for the journal, but never moves positions or balances: the broker's snapshot already holds them, so nothing is counted twice; repeats are dropped by `trade_id`. A broker that reports them says so (`BrokerCaps::external_fills`); for it, `rebuild` first applies what the broker has queued (fills before the positions they made), and adds no reconciled fill for orders adopted from a snapshot (their executions already came as external fills). External fills for an account not added yet are held (10k) and applied when it joins. Such a broker lists an account only once its positions are known and from then on reports every execution on it once, including those made while disconnected (after the reconnect), and none from before it was listed. Every request reaches the broker through `Broker::request_in_context` with a `RequestContext`: `source`, `origin` (`Origin::of`: `Rule`/`Api`/`Strategy` automated, the rest manual) and the issuing `PaneContext` (pane contract, `ContractSelection` follows-product or pinned, the resolver's `front_month`), set by `submit_from_pane` (`TradingHandle`/`TradingCore`); plain `submit` sends `pane: None`. The default implementation calls `request`, so brokers that don't need it (paper) ignore it. Latency: click → `Broker::request` is logged as `tracing` target `lines::latency` (`click_to_sent_us`) and kept in a 4096-sample ring (`TradingHandle::latency`).
- Strategies (decision 0025; `lines-trading/src/strategy.rs`, `core/strategy_gate.rs`, `lines-strategy`): a strategy only acts through commands with `Source::Strategy(id)` (`Origin::Automated`), and the **core** enforces the rules. Each strategy command passes the strategy gate first, then prop rules, risk and guardrails as usual. The gate checks four things:
  - **State** (Disarmed / Armed / Paused / Stopped): only Armed may enter (a bracket, an order beyond what closes the position, a modify adding quantity). Every state may reduce: cancel its own orders, flatten its own contract, move its exits (a modify of an entry order, price included, is an entry), and place orders no larger than the position less what open orders and held bracket exits (each bracket once) already close. A strategy's Cancel All cancels only its own orders; its Flatten does too when there is nothing to close, but closing a position ends every order on that contract, at once where the broker doesn't honor reduce-only, and exits only once it is flat where it does (`core/flatten.rs`): a trader's stop left after the position is gone would open a new one. Repricing or resizing an entry is an entry; whether a single order is closing counts the other open orders and held exits on its side. **Exits skip every check below** (hours, max position, daily loss, rate).
  - **Scope**: the instance's own account, contract and orders, with at most one instance per account and contract.
  - **Limits** (`StrategyLimits`, all required): max position (worst case with open orders), max daily loss on its own fills (its own orders, plus anyone's Flatten fills up to what closes its own position), entries per 60 s (counted only when the core accepts them), and entry hours in a named time zone.
  - **Paper only**: `Broker::simulated()` is true only for the paper broker; live brokers and a venue's own Sim accounts keep `false`.
  - Who issues controls: only a person (`Origin::Manual`) arms. A strategy may pause or stop only itself, and may never add, remove or arm.
  - `StopStrategies` is never gated and acts at once. Every instance stops before any flatten.
  - **The core pauses** an Armed instance for any of these: a manual order action on its account and contract (any contract for account-wide Cancel All or Flatten; before the action runs, even if it is then refused; the order the person modifies or cancels is left to them); the account not Active, or a guardrail lockout; the broker disconnected; its daily loss reached. The trading service pauses every armed instance when the strategy host, having sent a heartbeat, sends none for `HEARTBEAT_TIMEOUT` (5 s) of **wall-clock** time (decision 0027: a fast replay moves the market clock minutes in a moment).
  - **Daily loss (decision 0027):** reaching it **flattens** the instance's account and contract (every order there ended (at once, or exits once flat: `core/flatten.rs`)), once per trade date, also when the host had paused it with a trade open; never after Stop or when a person's own order paused it. Arming is refused until the next trade date.
  - Commands the core issues itself (a pause's cancels, Stop with flatten, a rule's liquidation, the daily-loss flatten) take ids from `INTERNAL_COMMAND_IDS` (2^62) up, never an id a `TradingHandle` gave out.
  - The strategy host (`lines_strategy::host`) runs instances on its own thread. It feeds them their feed's batches (`listen`, attached before anything else), their level sources (`LevelFeed`, polled by revision), their core events (`concerns`, the one definition shared with backtests) and a timer, and sends their commands as `Source::Strategy(id)`. Its own pauses: a stale feed, missing or stale levels, a contract change, a panic (the instance is then never called again). It adds instances Disarmed and never arms.
  - Pausing or stopping cancels entry orders (`Role::Entry`, and singles that don't only close the position). Bracket exits and Flatten orders stay.
  - Nothing re-arms automatically. Armed state is never saved. Arming reconciles the instance's own books with the account (flat adopts flat; any other mismatch is refused: flatten first). Removing an instance disowns its remaining orders. An instance owns only the orders its own Place, Bracket and Flatten created, never orders its Cancel All or Flatten cancelled.
  - Strategies are pure (`lines_strategy::Strategy`: no I/O, no clock, `Ctx::now` passed in).
  - The strategy panel (`lines-app/src/strategy_panel.rs` pure, `strategy_view.rs`) reads instances through `StrategySource` (the host, or `DemoStrategies` in tests and `--strategies-demo` screenshots) and each state from the core's `Event::Strategy` (`TradeState::strategy`) when it has one: the core enforces it. HALTED is exactly `strategy::DAILY_LOSS`. Its Start, Stop and Stop all go straight to the core through `Trading::submit` (a manual source), never through the host, so they work if the host hangs; Stop and Stop all flatten (decision 0025's default; "Stop all, keep positions" is palette-only). Stop all is `commands::BINDINGS` Ctrl+Shift+R from any pane, drawn with `popover::always_live`, and strategy commands never key-repeat. Start has no key. - Guardrails (`lines-trading/src/guardrails.rs`, roadmap 8b, decision 0006): per account, all optional: max trades per trade date (a round trip flat → position → flat; partial fills don't count, a reversal closes one and opens the next), a losing streak (trade P&L net of every fee; below zero is a loss) → cooldown of N minutes, max daily loss in money (realised + unrealised, separate from prop rules), max position (worst case with open orders), no entries in the last N minutes before the session close, and a news blackout (below). A breach is a lockout (`Cooldown` until a time, `Locked` until the trade date ends) with a reason; denials are `RejectCode::Guardrail` with a short US-English message ("Cooldown: 3 losses in a row, 12 min left"). Exits are never blocked: Flatten, Cancel All, cancels and orders no larger than what is left to close; a modify that adds quantity is checked as an entry. `Command::SetGuardrails`: tighter fields apply at once, looser ones with the next trade date; nothing clears a lockout early. Trade dates from `disk::partition::trade_date` of the account's `day_product`; a fill counts toward the trade date of its own timestamp (the broker's fill time), not the time the core processes it. `Event::Guardrail { account, state, reason, until }` on every change, its end included (the core wakes for it). Saved per account in the trading file (`SavedAccount.guardrails`, format v2); a broker venue's accounts (no trading file) in `<config dir>/paper/<extension name>.guardrails.json` whatever the data source, replays included (`TradingSetup::venue_dir`; `persist::GuardFile`, its own version 1, per account the tracker plus the trade date and its opening balance, so today's loss survives a restart; a newer or unreadable file is set aside and reported, and the run starts from settings), written by the venue's service (`TradingService::start_venue`) at once on a lockout or its end, else at most once a second, and holding saved entries of accounts not joined yet; `TradingCore::set_join_guardrails` restores each when the broker lists it (`add_accounts`), then asks for its settings like `SetGuardrails`, and reports a restored lockout. Defaults: `settings.json` `guardrails` keyed by account name (or id), asked for at every start and edited in Settings › Risk (each change is submitted as `SetGuardrails`; a loosening asks first and says it takes effect at the next trade date). Prop rules change the same way (`Command::SetRules`, `RulePreset::tightest`): what is tighter applies at once and checks the next order (orders already sent keep the rules they were checked against), what is looser waits for the next trade date, during a breach too; the account size is the account's starting balance; a tighter rule the account is already past (today's loss beyond the new daily limit, equity beyond the new drawdown, where the drawdown is the whole rule: amount, mode and lock, since a higher lock moves a locked threshold, more contracts open or working than the new cap) is refused with the reason unless that rule is already breached, so a setting never breaches, liquidates or blocks the account's own exits; `SetRules` publishes the account's view without checking breaches (the next equity update does, with the real balance); `SetGuardrails` checks today's loss against a tighter daily loss at once (a lock, which never liquidates nor blocks exits); at startup settings are sent only after the rebuild has restored the real balance, on a clock other than the wall's (a replay) both startup rebuilds and the settings run at `TradingCore::BEFORE_DATA` (`UtcMillis(0)`, what the service's market clock reads before data), and nothing at or before it rolls a trade date: rolling at the wall's date, or 1970's, would make the first data's date a new one and bring in waiting rules early, and refusals are reported like a setting that doesn't resolve; a new trade date closes under the old rules before waiting ones take over; never from a strategy. Looser rules still waiting are saved with the account (`SavedAccount.pending_rules`, optional). The account's view (`AccountView.limits`, optional) carries the rules and guardrails in force and those waiting, so the UI shows both. `settings.json` `prop_rules` (optional, keyed by account name, else id): a preset by name plus overrides of single values (`PropRulesDoc`), sent at every start; one that doesn't resolve is reported and left out. Screenshot scripts: `guard trades N | streak L/M | loss X | position N | close M | news warn|block|flatten`.

- News blackout (`lines-trading/src/news.rs`, roadmap 8b): `NewsBlackout` (action warn / block / flatten, `before_min`/`after_min` default 2, high importance unless `include_medium`) is an optional field on `Guardrails` and on `RulePreset`; the core uses the tighter of each part (`TradingCore::news_rule`), and tighten-now / loosen-tomorrow applies to it like every guardrail. It is checked in the core's guardrail path, on the core's clock (market clock in replays), never in the UI. Exits always pass (Flatten, Cancel All, cancels, orders that only close). The window is `[release − before, release + after)` on the products the release `affects`. Flatten: the core wakes at each window start (`next_wake`) and flattens each affected position once per window with a `Source::Rule` Flatten; entries stay refused for the window. A blackout is not a lockout: it raises no `Event::Guardrail` and no `GuardState` change (the UI reads the same schedule, `econ_ui::schedule()`, and the rule in force from `AccountView.limits`, which is what `TradingCore::news_rule` decides from). A flatten the core refuses is retried on later ticks in the window. A calendar that can't answer is never clear: no schedule, a date from `now − after` to `now + before` outside the coverage of a release the rule counts (at least its importance; Missing), or a `version` more than `STALE_AFTER_DAYS` (60) old (Stale) refuses entries under block and flatten (warn refuses nothing) but flattens nothing. Only new entries and quantity increases are checked: an entry order resting from before the window may fill in it, and moving its price is not checked (flatten closes such a fill on the next tick; block doesn't). The schedule (`lines_core::econ`, bundled `lines-feed/data/econ-calendar.txt`, format 1) holds official sources only (BLS, BEA, Federal Reserve), times in America/New_York converted with the bundled tzdb; a local time in a DST gap or fold is refused at parse; every line says `verified` or `unverified`. UI (phase 2): every reader goes through `econ_ui::schedule()` (the bundled schedule, or a screenshot's `--econ-calendar`), and the paper and venue cores are given the same one, so a chart, the trade panel and the core never disagree. The rule shown is the core's in force (`AccountView.limits`, the tighter of the guardrail's and the prop preset's, `econ_ui::rule_of`). The trade panel's status line shows a release within 30 minutes that moves the pane's product (`Scheduled release: CPI in 14:32`), a window in force as a strip with the time left, and a calendar that can't answer under Block or Flatten; while either refuses entries, the switch says why (`news blackout`; `calendar stale`, or `no calendar` when it doesn't cover the date or none is loaded) and entries dim (exits, Flatten and Cancel all never do). Charts mark each release that moves their product (medium and up) with a dashed line and a tag at the foot of the plot (`econ_layer::EconLayer`), up to the plot's right edge (a release ahead of the last bar shows in the margin): high filled in `warn` with `on_warn` text, medium outlined; tags that would overlap stack up to three rows, past which the line alone marks a release. Settings › Risk edits the rule per account (`guard.news`, `guard.news_before`, `guard.news_after`, `guard.news_medium`) like the other guardrails: tighter now, looser at the next session.
- Paper broker (`paper.rs`): deterministic, I/O-free, driven by `market` (feed batches via `lines_feed::listen` and `FeedBridge`) and `poll(now)`. Acks after `latency_ms` (default 25). Rejects off-tick prices, non-positive qty and orders while the product's session is closed. Fill models for resting limits: trade-through (default: a trade strictly through the limit), touch (a trade at the limit), queue estimate (behind the book's size at the price; trades at the price and a shrinking level reduce the queue). Several orders never fill more than a trade printed. Market orders and marketable limits walk the book; liquidity taken at a level isn't offered again until the level grows past it or goes (a quote or rebuild showing the same size is the same liquidity: paper errs pessimistic, 0005); no book → last trade; neither → a market order is cancelled (nothing would ever show it a price). A market order's remainder (a thin or one-level book, as a top-of-book feed gives) is never cancelled for want of liquidity: it stays Working with its remaining quantity and takes, first in line and at their prices, the book on its side once each batch is in (never from an emptied book, never mid-rebuild), each trade at or beyond the inside on its side (up to what the print has left after stops it triggered: a print at the bid never fills a buy, nor any print with no inside on its side) and each fresh option quote, until done or cancelled. Its session's close ends it (a day order, never filled at the next open; checked against the session it was placed in, so a replay's jump past the close ends it too). A stop filled at the print (no book on its side) takes its share of the print before resting limits see the rest (a print of 3 that fires such a stop for 2 leaves 1). Reduce-only, it still shrinks with the position, and like a Flatten's it takes none of the exits' cover (they still protect what it hasn't closed); a plain market order no larger than the position it opposes (a close) turns reduce-only when it starts working on, so an exit filling meanwhile can't leave it reversing the position. It never fills at the next open itself; a fired option Stop whose order the close ended sends a new one at the next session's fresh quote. While a Flatten's order works on, the position bracket places no new legs (existing ones stay), and a fired option Stop waits on it without a note of its own. Stops trigger on trades at or through the trigger. Fees per quantity unit per side, by product. Brackets and OCO are held broker-side (exits Acked until the entry fills, sized to the fill; an exit fill cancels or shrinks the sibling) and saved in the broker state, so they survive restarts.
- Trade journal (`lines-trading/src/journal`, roadmap 8c): a trade is one round trip per account + contract, flat → position → flat, by `guardrails::RoundTrip` (the one definition, shared with the guardrails' counts); a reversal closes one trade and opens the next, its fill's quantity split and its fee split pro rata (remainder to the closing side). Realized P&L comes from the journal's own FIFO lots (exact; right for reconciled fills too), fees from the fills. Fills are keyed by `trade_id` within a run: a fill seen again (reconciled twice, delivered twice) never counts twice; never across runs (paper fill ids restart when the paper file is reset, and the core never re-emits an earlier run's fills). Trade ids are unique (`<first fill id>@<entry ms>`, `#n` on a collision in a file), so a new trade never replaces a saved one. MAE/MFE: the worst/best of realized-so-far + open P&L (before fees) while open, from the market batches the paper broker sees (trades and last prices) and the fills. Everywhere it is shown or exported, money is the whole position and a tick number is per contract; MAE/MFE ticks are `Trade::excursion_ticks` (those dollars at the largest size), never a price excursion from `low`/`high`, which include prices from before a later add. It runs on its own thread (`JournalShared`): the trading thread's `service::Observer` only sends it messages (market batches after the core; the core's events before each market batch and after each drain, so a trade opened earlier in a batch sees the prices after it), the UI reads published snapshots (an `Arc` pointer swap) and sends edits and exports as messages: no lock is shared with the core, none is held while formatting. Only a trade closing, the open count changing or an edit publishes; market data and the core's per-batch position reports never do (tested through the service). The file is opened append-only and re-read at open, so a torn tail is cut from the file as it is then, never a stale length. The app flushes it on quit. File `<journal>/<source>.jsonl` (`data_dir::Locations`: the data folder's `journal/`, or the config folder's on an install from before the data folder) (live sources only; replays and screenshots in memory): JSON Lines, header `{"journal":2,"source":…}` (v2: contracts may be equities or options; a v1 file reads as it is, and opening it for writing keeps `<stem>.v1.jsonl` and swaps in the same records under a v2 header, atomically), then `{"trade":…}` / `{"edit":…}` records, externally tagged on purpose (serde's internal tagging buffers content and can't hold `i128` money); append-only on its own thread, except a paper reset that clears an account's journal (the file kept as a dated `.jsonl.bak`, then rewritten whole beside it and renamed in); a torn last line is dropped on read and cut before appending; a newer version is left alone and not written. Open trades aren't saved: after a restart a position the journal didn't see open is seeded from the core's position report, marked `incomplete` and dated from the first fill it sees. The Journal pane follows `pnl_display` (off: no money or tick number in rows, the stats strip or the by-account and by-tag tables, whose lines are then ordered by name so the order doesn't tell the P&L either; those tables are money only, so in ticks mode too they show counts and win rates and order by name; CSV export is always complete) and formats rows only when the journal's revision, the display setting, the account list, the trade date, the date range or the group moved. Untagged trades group under an empty key, which no tag can be. The pane's money is never unmarked or marked paper by assumption: each account's label starts with its badge in words (PAPER, SIM, LIVE; `UNLISTED` when no current list has it, as after a restart before a broker connects), and the header's mark comes from the accounts that have trades, or with none yet from the accounts listed (`journal_view::view_mark`: PAPER only when every trade is on a listed paper account, else "LIVE + PAPER" and the like, most real word first so a narrow header clips the paper word). An id two venues share reads as the more real. A venue's watcher moves `trading::accounts_epoch` when the account list changes, and the pane's revision includes it, so any change repaints and re-reads. Trade snapshots (`journal_shots`): a saved journal only (`JournalShared::start_with_shots` drops the sender without a writer; on whenever saved, no setting; a screenshot keeps a journal only under the evidence flag `--journal-dir`), one per trade as it closes, never for one loaded from the file or seeded from a position. No account in any form: the file name is `<trade date>-<FNV-1a 64 of the trade id, hex>.svg` (the id can hold the account: broker fill ids) and the drawing gets the trade with its ids, account, tags and note blanked, so it can't write them. The request carries the account beside that trade only to look up its kind: the file says PAPER, SIM, LIVE or UNLISTED in words (a stored record never reads as another kind), never the account's id or name. A trade the bars stop short of keeps its true place in a labeled gap, never on the nearest bar. `Trade::screenshots` holds that bare name, never a path (a path holds the OS user name), and a name read back is opened only if it passes `is_shot_file_name` (a saved line can't point the OS elsewhere). Not in the diagnostics bundle: it takes neither the journal's lines nor its charts.

- OMS checklist (docs/inspiration.md, one test each in `tests/trading.rs`, `checklist_*`): a command `Reject` is always a **denial** (the core refused, nothing sent); a broker's refusal is `OrderState::Rejected` on a sent order, and the UI labels them DENIED / REJECTED. Cancel All covers submitted and acked orders. A cancel is never fire-and-forget (`core/cancels.rs`): one unanswered for 2 s goes again by itself, 3 sends at most, then `Event::CancelFailed` says so once (a WARNING flash: the order is still live, or its outcome unknown) and nothing wakes for it. While the broker is disconnected no cancel is sent and no send is spent: a Cancel or Cancel All is remembered and goes when it is back. A Flatten with a position to close is refused whole then: its market order can't go, and a remembered cancel would strip the stop on reconnect. Its automated callers wait: the news flatten isn't asked while down and goes on reconnect if its window is still open, the daily-loss close waits and closes once the broker is back if the loss is still past the limit (the day isn't marked closed before that), and Stop with flatten stops and answers with the refusal. A person's next Cancel, Cancel All or Flatten sends again with a new budget; a rule's or strategy's adds none. A rebuild keeps cancels on their way (a venue rebuilds on every revision, so clearing them would stop the resend), including for orders it leaves `Unresolved`, but a person's next command for an order the broker still lists goes at once (rules wait the 2 s, or rebuilds would burn the sends). A broker refuses a cancel with `BrokerEvent::Rejected` for an acked, working, partly filled or unresolved order with a cancel sent: the order stays as it is (`Working → Rejected` stays illegal, a live order isn't rejected), `CancelFailed` carries the reason, nothing resends on its own and a rule's cancel adds none (a sweep would ask forever), and a person's next command goes at once. `Rejected` for an order with no answer yet, or no cancel sent, is the order's own. A refused or exhausted cancel no longer counts as on its way out (`cancel_pending`). A Flatten order counts as closing its position while open or `Unresolved`, for 5 s since the last word on it, so a rebuild before the broker answers can't let a second Flatten reverse the position; a Flatten command sends a cancel to an unresolved one past that, or when the position reads flat, once (a cancel that got nowhere isn't asked again on every Flatten), and past 5 s replaces it. The paper broker answers a cancel for an order it doesn't hold with `Cancelled`, which ends an unresolved one. A fill during a pending cancel applies (Filled, or Cancelled with its filled quantity); a fill reported after an order finished applies without reopening it. Every fill carries the broker's stable `trade_id` and the core drops repeats (any path); reconciliation (`rebuild`) reports fills the broker made while we weren't listening as one `reconciled` fill with id `recon-<order>-<filled>`, so repeating it never double-counts. Overfill: `OverfillPolicy::Record` (default: the broker's quantity is the truth; `Order::overfill`) or `Reject`, both reported as `Event::Overfill`, never clamped. Accounts have a `TradingState` (Active / ReducingOnly / Halted); prop breaches set it from the preset's `on_breach` (default: daily loss → reducing only until the next trade date, drawdown → halted); reducing only admits orders that close at most the open position less what open orders already close, and marks them `reduce_only`; the paper broker shrinks reduce-only orders as the position shrinks and cancels them when it is flat. Risk adds `max_notional`, `max_submits_per_sec` and `max_modifies_per_sec` (separate windows); cancels and Flatten are never rate limited. Brackets are one-updates-other: an exit's remaining is the bracket's open quantity (entry fills grow both exits, an exit fill shrinks its sibling); with nothing open the sibling is held while the entry still works and cancelled once it is done. From the entry's first fill its exits are reduce-only (the paper broker shrinks and cancels them with the position, so a manual close, or the rest of the entry cancelled, leaves none larger than the position; while the entry still works, an exit with nothing to close is held instead of cancelled, so the entry's next fill is protected again), and the core cancels any exit with nothing to close (reduce-only, or a bracket exit whose entry is done) on a flat contract or on the position's own side; a bracket exit whose entry works is the broker's to hold. A broker's snapshot must carry an exit's `parent`: without it the exit counts as live. `Unresolved` = sent with no known outcome (after a restart or a reconnect whose snapshot lacks it): never drawn as working; the DOM header lists them.
- Position brackets (`core/position_bracket.rs`, `Command::SetBracketMode`, owner request): per account and contract, manual trades get exits per entry (default) or as **one stop and one target for the whole position** (`BracketMode::Position { stop_ticks, target_ticks }`).
  - **Opening:** when the position opens, the pair is placed through `PlaceExit` (reduce-only, OCO), at the offsets from the exact average fill price. Each price is rounded onto the tick grid toward the entry, so neither is ever further than its offset.
  - **Never at once:** a leg that would fill or trigger at once against the core's last bid, ask or trade is not sent. Switching the mode on for such a position is refused.
  - **Following the position:** every sync (after each command, tick and market batch) compares each leg's remaining quantity with the position and resizes it, keeping its price (a person may move it).
    - Resizes go straight to the broker (`BrokerRequest::Modify`), with no guardrail, rate or reduce-only-growth check: they are protection.
    - **The broker must cap a reduce-only order at the position**, as the paper broker does. This is required of any broker with `oco`.
    - A resize still unanswered is sent again a second later, three times in all, and then the miss is published once. A resize that races a fill settles at the position.
  - **Flat and flips:** flat cancels the pair. A flip cancels it, and the new side gets its own pair once those cancels are confirmed: a new pair is placed only while no other exit works on the contract, so exits never add up past the position.
  - **Legs that end:** a leg that ends while the position is still open is placed again, at its last price (a person's move kept) unless the market is past it:
    - filled (flat and reopened within one poll, or an add left over): the new position is placed at the offsets from its own average, and the count of tries starts again;
    - ended by the broker: placed again;
    - `Unresolved` (a reconnect without it): never assumed gone, never placed again;
    - while a Flatten's order is open on the contract, nothing is placed. The Flatten's order is never paired with an exit as OCO.
  - **Refused legs:** a placement the core or the broker refuses is tried again a second later, three tries per leg, each refusal published. Out of tries, it says so once. A new pair waiting more than 5 s for other exits to finish also says so.
  - **A person's cancel:** a leg a person cancels (× or Cancel All, recorded from the command) stays cancelled until the position is flat, and so does a leg whose kind they place an exit for while it is down. Exits they place are their own: never resized or adopted, so a scale-out order is never grown.
  - **Entries and second exits:** in this mode a person's bracket order loses its exits (sent as the entry alone), and a person's exit of a kind whose leg is working is refused.
    - Brackets from automated sources other than strategies (`Source::Api`) keep their exits. Their exits and the pair are both reduce-only, so the broker's cap applies to their total.
  - **Switching to position mode:**
    - existing exits that already are one stop and one target, each for the whole position on the closing side, are adopted as the pair, as after a restart (the app sets the saved mode again); a missing one of the two is placed;
    - any other working exits are refused unless `replace`, with the count in the message (`exits_replaced_by_position_bracket`, what the confirmation names); with `replace` they are cancelled first.
  - **Back to per entry:** the pair stays, as two ordinary exits.
  - **Strategies:** the mode is refused, and does nothing, while a strategy instance trades the account and contract, and a strategy can't be added while the mode is on. A strategy's exits are its own.
  - **App** (`trade_bar.rs`, `brackets.rs`): the trade panel's Bracket list has the switch **One bracket for the position** (palette: "Trade: bracket for the whole position / per entry").
    - The setting is saved per account and product symbol, as `bracket_presets.<account>.position` (optional; absent = per entry). It is saved only once the core accepts the switch.
    - A pane tells the core its saved mode (with its preset, or the first one, which becomes the pane's) when it first draws, and again after a new preset or another account or contract. The core adopts the pair that is already there.
    - The command is routed like an order: to the account's core, refused on a read-only account. Its note is silent, so a refusal isn't a DENIED flash; the panel answers it.
    - A refusal naming the exits switching would cancel becomes the panel's confirmation, which takes the editor's place. The confirmation keeps its account and contract and is dropped when the pane moves; "Replace N" sends `replace` for exactly those.
    - While position mode is saved on, any refusal (or no preset to take its distances from) marks the Bracket field "position !" ("pos !" narrow) in the warning color, and the list's row "(refused)": it isn't in force, and a click on the row turns it off (per entry, which always works). Turning it on again retries. Render treats a busy store as unknown, never as "off", so nothing is sent on it. The entries then carry the pane's preset. A refusal while turning it on from off is only the person's flash.
    - A pane that told the core Position for its account and contract tells it per entry once position mode is turned off (by any pane). An answer never overwrites a preset picked meanwhile. Clicks read the setting with a blocking read; render only tries.
    - Answers are read with `try_read`, never blocking a frame. A command unanswered for 10 s counts as lost.
    - The switch acts from a DOM's panel only (`chart_runs` excludes it). None stays clickable while it is on and says why it can't be chosen; the bracket key skips it.
- App trading (roadmap 7; `trading.rs`, `trade_bar.rs`, `dom_trade.rs`, `dom_orders.rs`): the `Trading` global holds a `TradingHandle`; UI actions only `submit` (a channel send stamped with the input event's `Instant`) and never wait. An order tag or a drawing under the pointer gets its own menu instead. Tags, their `×`, the position tag, its handles, edge markers and a tag's resize control scale with the UI zoom (never below 100 %: they are hit targets) from one `order_tags::Metrics` a frame; each laid-out box stores its own size and the pointer tests those stored boxes, so a hit always matches what was drawn.
- Settings (decision 0021, roadmap 6c; `settings_schema.rs` pure, `settings_view.rs` the window; at least 640x420, and a row's label column keeps `LABEL_MIN_W`: a control that doesn't fit beside it wraps below, a choice's chips wrap, a scope chip ends in "…" past `SCOPE_MAX_W`): one table of `Setting`s (key, section, name, description, scope, safety class, kind = validation, get/set on `Prefs`, default) drives the Settings window's rows and search, the palette (`Command::Setting`: a choice or toggle sets that value; anything else opens the window at it; settings that already have palette commands, such as P&L display and the latency HUD, add none) and storage; no setting has a hand-built form. Trading-affecting settings carry the TRADING mark. A change that loosens trading risk (`settings_schema::loosening`: a guardrail made looser or removed, an opt-in "disable trading" turned off, a larger default quantity) asks first, from the window, the palette, a reset or an undo, and the confirmation is shown for the account it applies to; a guardrail loosening takes effect at the next trade date, the others at once; a guardrail tightening applies now (the core's rule, unchanged). Ctrl+, opens the one window (or brings it forward). Sections: Appearance, Charts, DOM, Trading, Risk (guardrails, prop rules, lockout and breach states), Accounts, Data (the data folder, the store's on/off, budget and depth products: they apply after restart, see Data folder), Connections (the sources' cards, see Connections), Keys, Diagnostics (a bundle's `settings.json` has its `connections` map redacted whole); Risk rows read what the core reports each frame (in force, waiting, locked out; a busy cache keeps the last frame's); a shown window repaints when an account's revision moves, so it never shows a stale state while idle. A risk change the core refuses puts back the entries it wrote (unless rewritten since) and says why on the row.
- Connections (decisions 0030 §3–4 and 0035; `connections.rs`, `connections_view.rs`): every registered source has a card in Settings › Connections, built from what it describes (`SourceExtension::fields`; public code names no private connector). Secrets live only in the OS keychain (service `lines`, account `<source>.<field>`, or, with `Field::keychain`, the entry the connector reads itself, under its own account: the target on Windows, the service in the default keychain or collection on macOS and Linux, so saving replaces its item): never in `settings.json`, workspaces, logs or diagnostics. Saved secrets are never displayed (decision 0035): the window gets only "Configured", where the secret comes from (keychain, or an overriding environment variable) and its last 4 characters (all hidden under 9); a field's environment variable overrides the saved value. The page reads the keychain on a worker thread, never while drawing (startup reads it once, before any window, to leave out sources not set up); the cards' switches write `settings.json` directly (no undo entry), and every control is reachable with ↑/↓ and Enter; screenshots and the benchmark use an in-memory keychain and never touch the user's. A source missing a required setting isn't started (its card says "not set up"). A connector built for another `extension::API_VERSION` is refused at registration.

- Window footer (`footer.rs` pure, `workspace::footer_bar`): three zones in every main window, always shown. **Safety** (left), in a fixed order, never hidden, folded or hideable: the most real account among the armed DOMs as a word (else among all DOMs; LIVE over SIM over PAPER; a broker account not listed yet reads LIVE; no word without a DOM), trading on with how many DOMs are armed (every window and tab, hidden panes included, counted each frame), feeds that aren't live (silent while their market is open, or connecting, reconnecting, failed or stopped; an ended replay is not one), REC with its time and Stop recording, Restart to update, and a notice that needs action (reports, a loud update word, a trigger that can't fire). Narrow, each shortens its words ("ON 2", "! 1 stale", "REC Stop") and never drops; the full words are in its tooltip. **Activity** (center): one line, a static glyph and a whole percent (a Download days run, a data-folder copy), else "Update available". **Links** (right): open positions, hidden and popped-out panes, crosshair sync, a trigger's quiet word, and "Layout: <name>" (never the bare name, which read as "trading is on"). `footer::fit` folds links into ⋯ from the last first, then shortens the activity, then the safety words, then drops the activity; once links have folded a narrower window never shows more; and when even the short safety words don't fit, they wrap in order onto more footer rows (the panes above give up the height), with ⋯ last, never clipped (tested at every width from the widest item, at 100 % and 150 %, with the drawn items' non-text widths counted). Feeds and background work are gathered once a second with the trigger watch (`footer::gather`), never waiting on a feed's lock (a busy store keeps its last word).

Throughput and latency budgets live only in `#[ignore]`d perf tests.

- In-app updates (decision 0031; `lines-app/src/update/`): only interactive runs check (`update::start`, after the windows open), never tests, screenshots, bench, backtests or headless runs, and never with `--no-update-check`, Settings › General › Updates: Off (no request at all), a build without the project's public key (`update::verify::PUBLIC_KEY`: the owner's key, ID `B11DD18F53C6E999`, pinned by a test), or a system other than Windows (`manifest::supported`: a macOS update must replace the whole bundle, not built yet). The check is one GET of the public releases list (no identifiers); a release offers itself only through its `update.json` manifest, and only within this build's channel and edition (`manifest::pick`). A download runs only after its SHA-256 and minisign signature verify against the compiled key and the signature's trusted (signed) comment names this very release, `lines <version> <edition> <channel> <target>` (`manifest::trusted_comment`: the manifest isn't signed, so this is what stops an older or other-edition signed file being offered as new), checked again at the restart press; anything else is discarded, reported on the status line, and never run. Restart to update and Roll back wait while any listed account has a position, a working order or an armed trigger, or a strategy runs (`hold::hold_reason`), read from the trading cache and the strategy source at the press itself, with or without the updater running (a busy cache refuses; the status line shows the same, refreshed each second). The press only records what to do (`update::Pending`); after this run has quit and flushed every save (`update::relaunch_if_requested`), an update verifies the staged file once more and copies the saved files (settings, workspaces, paper and guardrail files, drawings; not the store, recordings, journal or logs) to `<backups>/<old version>/` (`install::Dirs`, from `data_dir::Locations`: paper files kept as `paper/…` and restored to the paper folder, the rest to the config folder), renames the running executable aside (`<stem>.previous<ext>`) and copies the verified one in its place, and a rollback puts the previous executable back and restores that backup, so no quit-time save can write over what a rollback restored; then the executable in place starts with the same arguments. This relies on `run` returning only after the app (and so the `Trading` global, whose drop joins the trading core after its last save) is dropped. Staged downloads this version has passed are removed at start. `update-state.json` (versioned) counts the new version's starts until one has run 30 s; two failed starts offer the rollback, which restores that backup with the executable. Tests sign with a key generated at run time; no private key is ever in the repository.
