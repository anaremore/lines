# Known gaps

- Display time zone: "System" is the OS zone when it is chosen or at startup; an OS zone change while Lines runs shows after a restart (or choosing it again). Trade snapshots already drawn keep the zone they were drawn in (each names it). "ET" names US Eastern all year, so the hour repeated when DST ends (01:00-02:00 on the first Sunday of November) shows twice with the same label; `--time-zone` sets the zone for that run only, and the Settings row still shows the saved one.
- UI zoom (`ui_zoom`) doesn't scale: the chart's order strip (its buttons are the pointer's geometry, `chart_trade::strip_box`), drawing tags, the levels', profile's, footprint's and economic calendar's chart labels, the option chain (its header's height zooms, its text, row strip, ticket dock, armed rows, footer and ladder don't: its width rules are at 100 %) and the chart's order-group list, and window minimum sizes. A test (`tests/zoom_text.rs`) fails on any other literal text size the zoom misses (`text_size(px(..))`, `Glyphs::fixed`, a fixed size given to `shape_line`/`shape_sized`); these are its listed exceptions. A fixed-size container around zoomed text isn't caught by it, nor a size passed through a variable. A drawing note's text (being typed and placed) stays at 100 %, as drawings do; chart legends keep their 100 % maximum width, so they truncate sooner when zoomed. At 80 % some secondary text is below the type scale's 11 px (the trader's choice; hit targets keep 20 px). Above 100 % a pane holds fewer 100 % units, so the DOM's "shows what fits" rules drop the same rows they drop in a smaller window at 100 % (1600×900 at 150 % lays out like 1067×600 at 100 %). Zoom combined with DPI 2.0 is unverified locally (screenshot DPI can't be forced on Windows).
- Reset paper account: a reset waits while its flatten is working; it is dropped (and says so) when the flatten ends without closing the position or a new order is placed, never left to go off later. A restart while it waits drops it silently (the flatten order is saved, the waiting reset isn't). The trading file's `.bak` is taken when the reset is asked for (the last save then, at most a second old); a reset asked twice keeps two, and a reset later dropped leaves its `.bak` too. Trade snapshots of a cleared journal's trades stay in `journal/shots/`. An armed confirmation's label in the account menu stays until the menu repaints after its 5 s.
- Real CQG connection never exercised. Not implemented: session restore, variable tick tables, quote corrections (counted only), tick-arrival→presentation latency (the latency HUD measures receive → the start of a chart's paint, decision 0001). Decision 0013 gaps: no live source of prior-session volume yet, so feeds use the calendar rule; expiry/roll calendars ignore exchange holidays (provider expiry dates win; session calendars do model them); continuous series are not shown in the UI yet; the Rithmic symbol rule belongs to the private connector (decision 0014).
- Screenshot DPI cannot be overridden on Windows (GPUI reads monitor DPI); `--scale` asserts it instead. Screenshots use the OS UI font, so they differ across machines.
- Download days (decision 0030 §8): a contract a live feed is storing (here or in another Lines) is refused until its panes close, and a live feed that starts while a day of its contract downloads stores nothing until its next try after the download ends (every 10 s of feed time), then only that connection's trades (no bar index or coverage claims until its next connection, so that stretch isn't counted in the stored order flow). A day with any segment is never downloaded, so a day partly recorded live stays partial. Downloaded days are never evicted, so they can hold the store over its budget (older live days go first; the dialog warns when new days may pass it). Cancel remaining and closing the window let the day in flight finish and be stored and start no other; quitting Lines mid-day loses that day (cleaned up and fetched again next time). Stop now or a failure after the request discards the day, so a retry of a paid day is billed again (the provider bills the request; nothing partial is kept); the dialog counts such days as charged. A stop takes effect at the provider's next chunk; while the Databento gateway is still preparing a large answer that can take up to its 10-minute wait. Downloaded (pinned) days can't be unpinned or deleted from Lines yet (a way out is a follow-up). Downloaded top of book can keep a stale side after a book clear (mbp-1 `R`: Lines' best bid/ask has no "none"). A scratch folder left by a crash is swept only on the next download of that contract. A day with no market data in its session is not stored. Progress shows the bytes received (compressed) against the estimate's size (the provider's uncompressed measure), so it reads low. The Databento connector (private) refuses a date after the contract's expiry, or more than 5 years (quarterly products) or 9 years (others) before it, before pricing (a rule from the listing cycles, not each product's real first listing date); its downloads have no total time limit, but the answer must start within 10 minutes and no read may stall for 2 minutes. The dialog takes typed dates and a symbol; it has no calendar or picker yet, and the replay bar's Open recording menu doesn't link to it yet (`download_view::open` is the hook). Estimates and downloads were checked with screenshots and tests, not a live pointer (owner checks).
- Store: segments have no resync marker, so a corrupt block mid-file cuts the rest of that day (logged with the byte count). Provider history isn't in segments, so replaying a stored contract rebuilds bars only where trades were stored. The UI doesn't show store usage yet (defaults are in `--help`).
- Indicators: VWAP is exact where trades cover whole buckets (live, a replay, or the local store's trades replayed at startup for the newest stored sessions, `--orderflow-sessions`, default 2); elsewhere (provider history before that, an outage between launches, a coverage hole) it uses bar typical prices and is flagged approximate to the next reset. The WIT plugin world is designed (doc on `lines_indicators::Indicator`), not built (roadmap 10b).
- Volume profile: from counted trades only (live, replayed, or stored and replayed at startup for the newest stored sessions), not history bars (no per-price volume in them). Without the store (or beyond its coverage) it is partial (`partial from HH:MMZ`); a hole inside the range (an outage between launches) is labelled `gap HH:MM–HH:MMZ` (the first, `+N` more), but holes under a minute of open market are not shown. Readability at DPI 2.0 is by construction (device-pixel snapping) but was not checked here (this machine is DPI 1.0). `ProfileConfig` is saved per chart pane in the workspace file. Off by default.
- External levels: a level source that learns its products from a live service serves, in a replay, only its baseline products and those with recorded batches (`runtime::replay_recorded`); without `--replay-levels` (or in a screenshot of an unrecorded replay) only its baseline, shown "not recorded". No settings window yet (decision 0021): sources get schema defaults or values set with `levels::runtime::set_settings`. Official builds register sources with `lines_app::Extensions::with_level_source` (decision 0020; `run` forwards them to `levels::register`). A levels replay only matches a market replay recorded in the same session (batches are stamped with the live wall clock). No hotkeys for level toggles (pane menu and palette only). Level types (decision 0026): a source's recommended colors are user-independent source data, so the editor's warnings judge only colors the user picks; a source gives a fixed color that doesn't read on light a light-theme counterpart (`TypeStyle::light`), but a level's own row color (types kept at `TypeStyle::OWN`) is whatever the source sends. The editor's color row says "Recommended" for a type left alone even while the source-wide Lines or Zones color applies to it. The opacity chips offer 100/75/50/25 %, so a recommended opacity between them shows no chip selected. A zone type's fill under the candles is still held back by the 3:1 rule (a recommended 20 % grey shows at about 18 % on dark). With many types the editor's grouped tab strip is pinned above the body and scrolls on its own past a cap on a short pane (no scrollbar). Labels sit in a gutter right of the newest bar, sized from the whole level set, which takes plot width from the bars beyond the view's own right margin: when it appears, widens or goes (the levels, a label setting or the zoom changed) the bars shift by what the margin didn't cover; on a narrow pane it takes up to 40 % of the plot, and a pane too narrow for even one letter and a price shows no level labels; a time-axis drag (or wheel zoom) while the view's right margin covers part of the gutter isn't exactly anchored, since changing the bar width changes how much of the gutter the margin covers; a long name is cut with "…" (the full name isn't shown anywhere else yet); a VWAP-relative level's label is its name only; a label too wide for its room shows as "name… : price" (the name cut, never the price), and isn't shown when even one letter and the price don't fit (a label's cut for its room is made at its first paint, so until then it holds its room on the chance that a cut fits, and a label that turns out not to fit even cut gives its room back the next frame); close labels give way (the later one isn't drawn), never stacked side by side; a label on the last price's row is covered by the last-price line and tag, not dimmed. Level styles are app-wide by source, so a template or chart default no longer carries a level source's look, and a chart that had its own saved per-source style different from the app-wide one shows the app-wide one (its own is dropped at its next save; only a source with no app-wide style takes a chart's). A label's hide/show state is kept per level while the map lasts (a level scrolled off and back keeps its last decision's hysteresis), but a new map starts it over (a series-only push keeps it), so a source's refused refresh can show a label its neighbor was holding back. The studies menu's Kinds rows show kind slugs, not type names.
- Recording from the app: a start reconnects each live feed once (its data resumes after the logon and snapshot, as after any reconnect), so the file holds a whole session. Recordings hold one contract each, so a window with several symbols writes one file per feed, and a front-month roll ends that feed's recording. Popped-out pane windows have no footer, so they show no REC indicator (the main window does). A feed opened after Start isn't recorded until a Stop and a new Start (its level sources are). `--record-levels` from launch records on its own: the app's Start and Stop leave it alone. A `--record` recording from launch shows as REC and the app's Stop ends it (and Start waits for it to end). Popped-out pane windows have no Recording row in their pane menu (the palette and the main window's menu do). Level recordings start with each source's newest batch, not its history. There is no list of recordings in the app yet ("Open recording…" is decision 0030's later item); the files are in the data folder's `recordings/`. Start, Stop and the indicator were verified with unit tests, a mock feed and screenshots, not a live pointer (owner checks).
- External lower-pane series: a workspace or settings file holding one (`"kind": "external"`) can't be read by a build from before it (set aside as unreadable, defaults load), as with Volume. A series updates as often as its source refreshes (a source fetching history once a minute adds a point a minute); a replay shows what the source's recorded batches held. Values print with one decimal from 10 up and two below, whatever the source's precision. The panes' crosshair readout and × were verified with unit tests and screenshots of synthetic series only, not a live pointer or a real service (owner checks).
- Volume study: a workspace or settings file (chart defaults, templates) holding a Volume (`"kind": "volume"`) can't be read by a build from before it (that file is set aside as unreadable and defaults load), as with cumulative delta: the optional-field rule covers new fields, not new study kinds. Lower panes' heights belong to their positions, not their studies: removing or hiding the top pane's study moves the next one up into its height. Split by buy/sell reads the counted trades (as the footprint), so bars before them (history, or older than the stored order flow) show their whole total as "no side". On tick charts the split reads each bar's own trades; time bars read the per-minute footprints, kept as long as the chart's 1-minute bars. The split merges each visible bar's per-minute footprints every frame under the store lock (no allocation); on daily or weekly bars that is about 1,440 minutes a day per bar, not yet measured on a busy session. A chart shows at most 4 lower panes; more lower-pane studies wait for room. The overlay's bars sit under the candles and are held back to keep them readable, so a strong custom color shows fainter there. Dragging the lower panes' dividers and the crosshair readout over Volume were verified with unit tests and screenshots only, not a live pointer (owner checks).
- Footprint and cumulative delta (roadmap 10e): from counted trades only (live, replayed, or stored and replayed at startup), so bars the store doesn't cover are candles and delta over them is a bar-level tick-rule estimate (flagged approximate). The startup replay takes about 0.25 s per busy trade date (590k trades, release build) on the feed thread before it connects: live data starts that much later, and a longer `--orderflow-sessions` costs proportionally. Segments written before the store kept only counted trades may hold a reconnect's re-sent trades, which this replay counts twice (tick bars too: they count the same trades); they leave the default lookback after two newer stored sessions. After a crash, trades stored in the last ~30 s before it are not yet claimed complete; if a later connection's history snapshot is older than them and the provider re-sends them, live counts them twice and the store keeps both (the replay matches live, both high). One cumulative delta per chart (one sub-pane). Trades without an aggressor side are flagged in the footprint (`?N` cells, a legend count) but cumulative delta still ignores them without a flag. The sub-pane divider has no resize cursor on hover (the drag works); hover, drag and wheel in the sub-pane await the owner's live check. A workspace, template or `chart_defaults` holding a cumulative delta (indicator kind `cum_delta`) can't be read by a build from before it (that file is set aside as unreadable and defaults load: the optional-field rule covers new fields, not new kinds). Footprint and cumulative delta are set in the studies menu only (Ctrl+I, the palette's and pane menu's "Studies…"; screenshots: `--study-set`), with no direct keys.
- Pane headers narrower than their tested minimum (chart 700 px) clip the left group at the hover controls' gutter: the account chip loses its ▾ and the PAPER badge can be cut off, so paper vs live is not visible in the header there. Header dropdowns open from the chip's visible part.
- Latency HUD (roadmap 6b): shown in chart headers only (DOM and T&S record nothing and show nothing; receive → paint is per chart). It shares the header's right-hand text: a chart under 1200 px shows p99s only, and a very narrow one still truncates it from the end (the clock warning is a badge on the left and stays). Feed latency needs the trade's exchange time: sources whose trades are stamped at receipt (the mock) read `n/a`. The clock bound includes the link's transit, so a slow link reads as a clock ahead; it is shown for live wall-clock feeds only (never for replays) and has not been seen against a live Coinbase/Kraken feed here (awaiting the owner's check). Order latency comes from the paper broker (its ack is simulated, labeled `ack (sim)`); for real brokers `at − created` mixes the broker's clock with the core's, and a broker that acks bracket exits only when the entry fills, or replays acks after a rebuild, would need its own rule. After the wall clock steps back (NTP), a live feed records nothing until the clock passes the newest recorded second again. Paint latency is measured to the start of this chart's paint, not to the GPU's presentation.
- Popovers: the dismiss rule (outside press closes and is consumed, Esc, drafts, one open at a time) is tested as pure logic and shown in screenshots, not with a live pointer (owner checks). A popover's backdrop covers its own window only: a press in another window (a popped-out pane) does not close it and acts there as usual (orders included), though opening a popover there does close it. While a popover is open the window under it gets no hover or wheel (no crosshair). The bracket editor and the symbol picker don't follow the rule yet (the picker keeps its own close-on-click).
- Trade-built charts (roadmap 9b: tick `500t`, volume `500v`, range `10r`, seconds `30s`; what follows says tick bars for all four): tick bars count the same trades as the tape and VAP, so they reach back as far as the stored order flow does (`--orderflow-sessions`, default the newest 2 stored sessions; a restored tick chart gets them at startup); earlier trading has no tick bars and they say "tick bars from MM-DD HH:MM ET" (the display zone) in the plot. A rebuild that can't read every counted trade again falls back to the tape's oldest print (the last 8192 trades), labelled the same way: without the warm start (`--orderflow-sessions 0`, nothing covered) when the store holds trades from before this process on the tape's trade date, when this process has been counting since an earlier trade date, when a stored day was evicted since startup, and live without a local store. A hole in the counted trades (an outage between launches, a disconnect) is not labelled on tick bars, which continue across it (the profile and footprint label it). A frame that arrives late across a trade-date boundary is stored under the previous date's segment, after that date's other trades, so a stored rebuild there can order it differently from live (bars around the session change may differ by that trade's place). A session's last bar turns from forming to partial at the close on the feed's clock, but a chart repaints only when data arrives, so with no data after the close it shows forming until the next update. Footprint columns don't carry the partial/forming dashed box (the readout still says so). The DOM's bracket buttons use "t" for price ticks ("8/16 t") while the chart chip's "233t" means trades (lists and palette spell out "233-trade bars"); renaming the DOM labels is a separate DOM change. A replay rebuilds from its first frame. Live without a local store starts from the tape. VWAP and cumulative delta on tick bars are sampled at minute resolution (the engine's 1-minute point holding each bar's last trade), and a delta candle runs from the previous bar's sampled close. Bars that start in the same millisecond share a start time, so a drawing anchored there lands on the later one. A volume bar keeps the trade that reaches its size whole (never split), so it can hold more than its size; a range bar closed by a price gap spans fewer ticks than its size (no bars are invented across a gap); seconds bars have no bar for a slot without trades and show no "left" count (their clock closes them). Volume bars count whole units of the product's quantity, so on a coin `500v` means 500 coins and a fractional size can't be set. The readout gives a bar's size ("41 of 233 trades") for tick bars only; other kinds show their trade count. The chip's "N left" is in the kind's unit (trades, contracts, price ticks of range) without naming it. The state labels say "tick bars" ("building tick bars", "tick bars from …") on every kind. A session reset inside a seconds slot (a holiday halt mid-slot, or any slot size not aligned to the session's open or close, such as 2700s around a 22:00 UTC close; with trades on both sides) starts the new bar at the same slot start as the old one, so a time in that slot maps to the later bar, and a session's first seconds bar can start before its open. Switching a chart to tick bars (`ChartStore::acquire_ticks`) takes the store's lock with a blocking read on the UI thread, as switching timeframes already does for derived bars; the feed holds that lock only briefly (one frame, or at most 4096 held trades when a rebuild lands), so a switch can wait that long, never longer. Hover and the typed `500t` / `500v` / `10r` / `30s` entry await the owner's live check.
- Resizing working orders: orders in a group (a chart group tag, a DOM mark for several) can't be resized from it or from the group's list; zoom in until they split. A chart tag too near the plot's left edge has no control. Resizing a linked pair sends two modifies: if the core refuses one (a rate limit, say), the legs differ until the next resize, and the refusal shows. Repeated steps before an ack start from what the order last reported (+ + from 5 asks for 6 twice). Any pointer move arms the wheel, even a pixel of jitter while a tag sits under it. The controls, the wheel and typing await the owner's live check.

- Option chart's lines (decision 0029, PR 2): only beside a chart: the plain chain shows and cancels armed triggers but can't add or move one. On the plain chain the armed rows take their height from the table, so a trigger ending can re-center unheld rows. When SPY, Set Stop, Set Target, Arm and a trigger's Cancel have no key or palette entry (the ticket's Tab and Enter cover part of When SPY). More than 16 lines on an underlying: the rest are only in the armed rows. A line off the plot has no line or tag: its armed row says "off the chart". On the chain a line takes a press only between rows. Estimates use the quote's implied volatility with no rates or dividends, moved to read the mid at today's price; a quote without one shows no estimate. Pointer behavior awaits the owner's live check.

- Positions pane: positions only, no working-orders list yet. Its "Last" is read back from the core's open P&L (the price that P&L was taken at), not a quote of its own. A working order whose cancel was refused stops holding the wait, so the market order goes beside it, as it does beside an order placed during the wait. A plain order already `Unresolved` before the Flatten gets no cancel and no wait. A market order that went after a wait isn't recorded as a strategy's own, so its chart marker is missing (its P&L still counts). A close waiting when the broker disconnects or Lines quits doesn't go: the first shows a rejected order, the second nothing, and the orders it canceled stay canceled, a rule's Flatten included. A broker honoring reduce-only keeps a position's exits, which needs it to let a Flatten's market order close the position whatever exits are working (`BrokerCaps::oco`). A stop placed from the DOM is a plain order rather than a reduce-only exit, so a Flatten cancels it at once and a Flatten that then can't close (a halt) leaves that position without it; chart stops, brackets and triggers stay. Its cancel can also be overtaken: a stop triggering before the cancel lands, or a cancel the venue loses, could open a position after the Flatten closes it.
- Equities and options (decision 0028): the model, chain data, paper options in the core and the chain pane's ticket. Triggers on the underlying (decision 0029) work only while Lines runs with a fresh feed, a local trigger protects nothing while it's closed. Triggers and strategies never share an option: a trigger won't arm where a strategy is armed, paused or holds a position (by the strategy's own book, so one stopped holding a position the trader then closed elsewhere still refuses: remove it), and a strategy won't arm where a trigger is alive. A fired entry's resting limit doesn't count, like a person's resting order. An entry buys only and arms with no exits (arm them when it fills), its limit rests for 10 s if the ask moves away before it arrives (then it's canceled, though a fill can still beat the cancel, as with any cancel; after a restart inside those 10 s, or if the core refuses that cancel, it rests until a person cancels it: the timer isn't saved, and the refusal is said on the Entry's row, not tried again, while a broker's later refusal shows only as the cancel's own warning; one with no known outcome waits, never canceled while it's unknown, and is canceled once the broker says it's working and its time is up, or never if no answer comes), and a gap past its level ends it. Exits are armed for the whole position. After a restart a Target that was chasing comes back armed, its order left resting where it was until the level is reached again, and a Stop that was waiting for a bid needs a new print past its level. A Stop or Target's order may rest with the underlying's feed gone once fired; only firing needs the feed. On a replay the market clock runs on wall time between prints, so a gap over 10 s of real time reads as stale whatever the speed (firing itself uses market time). A quiet underlying counts as stale after 10 s, so a thin one can end an entry that a fresh feed would have fired. No live source supplies SPY or QQQ yet (a synthetic recording does: `cargo run -p lines-feed --example synth_chain`), so they open as unavailable on live sources; the chain pane keeps only its account across restarts (it starts on the soonest expiry, at the money, with no ticket and One-click off); the option order size is its own per underlying (`SPY options` in `order_sizes`), not the shares' size, and the Settings default still seeds it; a position on a strike scrolled out of the rows shows only by scrolling to it (another expiry's shows as a dot on its chip, among the 8 chips in view); on a replay that has ended, a quote can pass the stale limit with no repaint until the next change (Enter checks again, so nothing sends on it); a ticket left open past its expiry stays open and refuses; a close leaves plain working orders on that side working, and the ticket says so; the strip's one line holds what fits, the rest shown as `+N more` (Cancel for all of them still reaches them), and below about 300 px its one Cancel can clip; opening a ticket can recenter the rows if it cuts off the selected one (it sends nothing, and it names its contract; with One-click on, the rows hold); **Accepted for paper, each to close before live options:** a pending order looks the same as a working one on the rows; a row's Close and Cancel are drawn 16 px tall in 18 px rows (their target is the whole row height, out to the cell's edge); they act on the click that also focuses the pane (they can't open a position); a very large position or price can outgrow the Pos cell; a saved venue account not listed yet shows no account chip here. One-click's focus check reads the last frame, so a click that only reactivates the window after a repaint can still send; the ticket's price and size use the keys and chips, with no typed quantity; with One-click on the rows and the price row's slot stay as they were when it went on, so a newly listed strike isn't shown, the price row can sit a strike or two from the price (its tag and the in-the-money shading stay live), and if it was out of view it stays out, which in a pane under 560 px leaves the shading as the only sign of the price; keys move only among those strikes; turned on while a chain is still arriving it holds what had arrived, and Home takes it back to the money; all of them end when One-click goes off, on Home or on another expiry; its header shows 8 expiries around the shown one (a pane under 560 px shows none: the expiry is named on the table's first row and Left/Right change it, and a position on another expiry has no dot there; a wider one drops whole chips off the end, and the title names the shown expiry); with no strike selected and One-click off the rows recenter on the money as the underlying moves; past expiries stay in a running feed's chains until it restarts; a frame of option quotes alone keeps the underlying's feed from showing stale even if its own quotes stopped. The us-equity holiday table is unverified (from NYSE's published schedule as republished, 2027 partly by pattern). On an early-close day the whole equity session ends at 13:00 ET, though real after-hours trading runs to 17:00. Options have no hours of their own yet: an option key shares its underlying's product, so its session calendar is the equity's (04:00–20:00 ET), while options trade only in the regular session (to 16:15 ET for SPY and QQQ, except on their expiry day). The expiry calendar (SPY and QQQ list daily expiries) isn't modelled: an option key takes any date as its expiry, settling at the regular close that day. Everything keyed by product is shared by an equity and its options (drawings, crosshair price sync, paper fees, recent symbols), so a level drawn on an option chart would show on the equity's; nothing reaches this until a single option can be charted. Adjusted options (non-standard deliverables, other OCC roots) aren't modelled. A workspace, trading file or journal saved by this build is refused by older builds (copied aside or left alone, per each file's rules), even when it holds only futures. Paper accepts every short an account opens, uncovered calls included, where a real broker often rejects one by the account's options level: paper is looser here, and a real broker's message reaches the ticket's or strip's note, or a fired trigger's row, in its own words through the generic rejection path; Schwab's own messages are to be wired in with its connector, which doesn't exist yet. Two Entries armed on one short are each checked alone when armed, so the second can be refused when it fires (its row says why). An order with no known outcome counts toward crossing flat even if the broker dropped it, until the next rebuild settles it; Close still works. The ticket doesn't count an order with no known outcome toward crossing flat, so it lets such a sell through and the core refuses it in the same words. Refusing a grown order through flat reads as the added quantity (`selling 2 with the 1 already working`), not the new total. Paper options are an approximation (decision 0028): they settle only at expiry, in cash at intrinsic value, so there is no early exercise or assignment, no share delivery, no dividend effect and no pin risk; a short in the money is never assigned early. Settling needs the underlying's price and uses the last one seen when the clock passes the close, up to 24 hours old (a feed that died at 10:00 settles on its 10:00 price, with no warning), and a local clock behind the exchange can count a print just after the close. If there is no price from those 24 hours (the app wasn't running, no pane followed the underlying) the position waits and settles on the next price seen, not the real close; until then it stays open. Fills take a quote's whole side (quotes carry no size), with no slippage, and queue estimate behaves as trade-through. With no two-sided fresh quote a limit is refused, except a close at its own side of a fresh quote: a position whose bid is gone can't be offered out of, and Flatten's market order works on until a bid comes back (or the session closes), with its exits still working; it expires worthless at the worst for a long; a short stays open and pays intrinsic at settlement, with no cap. A reduce-only limit at its own bid or ask passes the far-from-mid guard however wide the quote (decision 0029); with no usable bid a long can't leave until one comes; the chain pane shows the Flatten working, with no note of why it hasn't filled, and says so if it is canceled with the position still open. A limit that rested instead of crossing (the quote had gone stale, or the session had closed) later fills at its limit, not at the better quote. Settling uses the underlying's last price the marks had when the clock passed the close: prints still in a batch straddling the close aren't counted. Quote age rests on the clock and the vendor's stamp: with stamps ahead of the clock (now counted as arrival time), a late quote can replace a newer one, and a chain's bad stamp seeded when the core starts following looks fresh; on a market clock, which moves on trades only, a quote can look older than it is after a long gap between trades, and one before the first trade is stale until the series quotes again (that error only refuses or rests orders). No live source supplies options yet; this needs closing before one does. The journal's excursions also count a late quote the core ignored. Book, bid and ask inputs on an option key skip the quote rules: nothing sends them while no pane follows a single option, and that has to close before charting one. A disconnected feed sends the core nothing, so a quote keeps filling until it passes its age limit (10 s same-day, 60 s later). Each quote is its own message on the trading thread, so a large chain snapshot delays commands queued behind it. The news rule doesn't cover SPY or QQQ: the economic calendar names no equity. Marks and markets of expired series stay until restart. The guard's settings live in the account's saved limits, with no Settings row yet; a quote counts as stale after 10 s on a series expiring today and 60 s on a later one (`risk::option_quote_stale_ms`), with no setting. **On a feed that sends only changes, a quiet series reaches that while its quote is still good: its order is refused and its Stop or Target waits until the next change, and an Entry firing then ends for good. Check this against the real feed before relying on a far-from-the-money or long-dated series.** Replay below 1x stretches the same gaps (`synth_chain`'s quotes are inside the limits at 1x and faster), and a synthetic recording made before these limits, at its old 5-minute cadence, refuses nearly everything: make it again with the `synth_chain` example. The session check still uses 16:00 ET, not the 16:15 close of SPY and QQQ options. A prop-rule account can't open options at all, rather than trading them outside its rules.
- Paper market orders on a thin book: a remainder on a product with no session calendar or one that never closes (crypto), or with the session check off, has no close to end it, and it is saved with the paper state, so it can fill after a restart, at the price of the day it fills; cancel it to end it. A print at a level the simulation already took can fill a remainder (the feed doesn't say whose liquidity a print was), which leans optimistic on a top-of-book feed. What was taken is forgotten when a snapshot's clear and its levels come in separate batches, and on a restart (it isn't saved): the rebuilt inside counts as new. A market order larger than the position (a reversal on purpose) keeps its rest plain, so an exit filling while it works leaves it further reversed than asked, as a resting limit would. A remainder walks the book before requests due earlier in the same poll (a cancel due before a coarse batch can come too late). The other way, a level that shrinks and grows back is counted as the same liquidity, which leans pessimistic.
- Reverse (`core/reverse.rs`): on a broker honoring reduce-only, orders that don't protect the position (an entry stop, a plain limit) and bracket legs the broker never reported reduce-only are cancelled as the close and opening go, not before (such a leg is gone too if the close then fails), so one filling in that moment adds to the new position. A Flatten pressed during a Reverse cancels the opening, but if the opening fills first the new position stays (Flatten again); an opening a rebuild left with no known outcome isn't cancelled by it, and a strategy's Flatten after the close filled cancels only the strategy's own orders. In the paper broker a person's market order sent while a Flatten's order works on a thin book counts as an entry, not a second close, so it can open the other side as a Reverse's opening does. The DOM refuses Reverse with no market price when a bracket preset is set, even in position mode, where the core doesn't use those prices. A Reverse counts as one submission for the order-rate limit though it sends two orders. A Reverse is refused while an order on the contract has no known outcome, until the broker settles it (a person can't cancel such an order; Flatten and a manual entry still work), an unresolved Flatten order included.
- Cancels (`core/cancels.rs`): a position-bracket leg isn't linked again when its cancel is refused or never answered, so that order stops being resized and a second leg may go beside it. A rule's Flatten (a daily-loss or prop-rule close) sends nothing to an order whose cancel was already refused or ran out of sends: the earlier WARNING is the only word, and a person's Flatten sends again. A refused modify arriving while a cancel is on its way reads as a refused cancel. An `Unresolved` order can't be cancelled by a person (only a Flatten's cancel reaches an unresolved Flatten order). Sierra DTC: it reports a refused cancel on a working order only as an alert, so that reads "no answer to 3 cancels" here; its own cancel bookkeeping doesn't merge resends. While a broker is disconnected: Stop all sends its Flatten and the refusal shows nowhere (a single Stop shows it), and the row reads STOPPED; a person's refused Flatten counts as taking over, so the daily-loss close then leaves that trade to them; a daily loss that recovers before the broker is back isn't closed; a news window that ends during the outage flattens nothing; a daily-loss close after an outage leaves the row's reason as the disconnect, not HALTED (Start is refused with the reason); a prop rule's liquidation is refused and isn't tried again on reconnect (the position stays open on a breached account until a person flattens); a fired Stop trigger's refused close shows its note until the next quote, then retries. An order the broker rejects after a cancel was sent reads as a refused cancel until a rebuild: acked, it stays "still working" until then; unresolved, nothing ends it (the DOM header lists it, and a Flatten's hold on it still ends after 5 s).
- Position brackets (`BracketMode::Position`): resizing a leg relies on the broker capping a reduce-only order at the position, as the paper broker does. `BrokerCaps` has no bit for it; `oco` is taken to imply it, and extension brokers aren't checked. The app applies a saved mode from a pane that draws, so an account and contract with no trade panel open keeps per entry until one opens; two panes on the same contract with different presets send their own (the last one wins, for the next position). The bracket's own messages (a refused leg, out of tries) are published by the core as `Source::Rule` command results, which the panel doesn't show yet: they reach the log.
- Paper trading on a source without depth (one level per side): a market order larger than the inside fills the inside and works on for the rest, taking later quotes and trades as they come rather than walking a book it can't see (see Paper market orders on a thin book). A triggered stop fills its remainder at the triggering trade's price, with no slippage beyond it. Limits rest and fill on trades as usual. The queue-estimate fill model assumes no queue at prices away from the inside.
